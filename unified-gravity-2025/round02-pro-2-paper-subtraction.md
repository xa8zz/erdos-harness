**Verdict:** the claim is **false** for the identifiable published theory matching your description, namely Mikko Partanen and Jukka Tulkki’s unified-gravity model, *Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model* (Rep. Prog. Phys. 88 (2025) 057802, DOI 10.1088/1361-6633/adc82e). The match is essentially unique: four commuting (U(1)) factors acting on a “space-time dimension field,” an eight-spinor formalism, a Minkowski-metric branch, and a TEGR/Weitzenböck reduction all appear together there. ([arXiv][1])

A Yang–Mills-style BPHZ proof would need the usual algebraic-renormalization input: one-loop counterterms must be **local integrated polynomials** of ghost number (0), with bounded dimension, modulo BRST-exact terms; ghost number (1) classes are the possible anomalies. This is the framework of Becchi–Rouet–Stora (1976, *Renormalization of gauge theories*, DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(76)90156-1) and Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux (1995, *Local BRST cohomology in the antifield formalism: I. General theorems*, DOI 10.1007/BF02099464; 1995, *Local BRST cohomology in Einstein–Yang–Mills theory*, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(95)00471-4). Barnich et al. also emphasize that ghost number (0) controls deformations/counterterms and ghost number (1) anomalies. ([ScienceDirect][2])

In the published model, the four gravity symmetries are explicitly **Abelian**. The gravity Faddeev–Popov determinant is (\det[(1/g'*g)\partial^2]), the gravity ghost action is (L*{g,\mathrm{ghost}}=-\hbar c,\bar c_g\partial^2 c_g), and the ghosts are then integrated out because they do not couple to the gravity gauge field. The renormalized UGM Lagrangian uses only multiplicative factors (Z_\psi,Z_m,Z_A,Z_H,Z_{g\psi},Z_{gm},Z_{gA}); its quadratic gravity term is still just the original TEGR-like two-derivative term plus ((Z_H-1)) times that same term, and the harmonic-gauge graviton propagator is (iP^{(D)}/p^2). So, inside the bare parameter family actually analyzed in the paper, the available quadratic graviton counterterms are only **two-derivative** ones. ([arXiv][1])

The obstruction already appears in the **one-electron-loop graviton self-energy**; the extra photon loop in the full QED+gravity version only reinforces it. The paper’s own 1PI formula has an ultraviolet pole with overall behavior ( \sim \kappa,p^4/\epsilon_{\mathrm{UV}}) multiplying transverse tensor projectors, while the paper’s own counterterm insertion for that 1PI function is (i(Z_H-1)p^2\hat P). A constant (Z_H) cannot absorb a (p^4) divergence. In the scaled variables (H_{a\nu}=\sqrt{\kappa},H'*{a\nu}) that the authors use when claiming a dimensionless coupling, the required subtraction is a new four-derivative gauge-invariant quadratic operator, schematically (H'*{a\mu\nu}\Box H'^{a\mu\nu}) or ((\partial_\lambda H'_{a\mu\nu})(\partial^\lambda H'^{a\mu\nu})). Under the standard 4D dimensions assumed in your problem, that is a **dimension-6** operator. It is not a redefinition of any parameter already present in the bare action. ([arXiv][1])

The paper’s attempted cure confirms the failure rather than removing it. It imposes
[
\Xi_{1L}\big|*{p^2=0}=0,\qquad \left(\frac{\Xi*{1L}}{p^4}\right)\Big|_{p^2=\infty}=0,
]
the second condition being justified there by “integrability” of the Newtonian-potential correction. Solving those conditions produces a “renormalization factor” (\delta Z_H^{(1)}) that depends explicitly on (p^2), (m_e^2/p^2), (m_e^4/p^4), and logarithms. That is not Yang–Mills-style field-strength renormalization. Read locally, its pole part is shorthand for a **new higher-derivative counterterm** absent from the bare action; read literally, its nonpolynomial momentum dependence would correspond to a nonlocal subtraction. Either way, the original action is not closed under renormalization. ([arXiv][1])

The advertised “dimensionless coupling” does not rescue power counting. The original gravity kinetic term is written with Einstein’s constant (1/\kappa). The paper then rescales (H_{a\nu}\to \sqrt{\kappa},H'_{a\nu}) and defines
[
E'_g = E_g\sqrt{\kappa/(\hbar c)}=\sqrt{8\pi\alpha_g},
]
with (E_g=c\sqrt{p^2}) and hence (\alpha_g = Gp^2/(\hbar c^3)). So the supposed dimensionless coupling is introduced only **after** a (\sqrt{\kappa})-rescaling and is explicitly **energy-scale dependent by definition**. That is not the fixed bare dimensionless coupling on which Yang–Mills power counting rests. ([arXiv][1])

The TEGR requirement fails as well. The paper’s renormalization analysis is explicitly in the **UGM** (exact-Minkowski) formulation, while **TEGRW** appears later as a separate reduction. In that TEGRW branch the gravity term is again (+\frac1{4\kappa}H_{\rho\mu\nu}S^{\rho\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}). The new four-derivative counterterm found above is not of that form, so the field-dependent geometric reduction is not stable off shell. This matches independent teleparallel work by Casadio, Kuntz, and Paci (2022, *Quantum fields in teleparallel gravity: renormalization at one-loop*, DOI 10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-10157-8), who conclude that one-loop matter divergences cannot be absorbed by the TEGR action itself and that a more general teleparallel action with extra free coefficients is needed. ([arXiv][1])

There is a second, weaker warning sign in the paper’s own treatment of the equivalence principle: it states that the one-loop contributions to the renormalization factors of the gravitational and inertial mass sectors are **not** equal and that these different renormalization factors are “necessary” for renormalizability. So equality of renormalized inertial and gravitational masses is being imposed as a renormalization prescription, not derived from the BRST/Slavnov–Taylor structure. ([arXiv][1])

Separately, the same authors later published *Extending unified gravity to account for graviton-graviton interaction* (arXiv:2507.07790), explicitly describing graviton–graviton interaction as a **necessary extension** of the original Minkowski-space formulation. That is not the main disproof here, but it is further evidence that the 2025 model was not already the full nonlinear gravity theory whose renormalizability was being claimed. ([arXiv][3])

So the disproof is explicit:

1. the one-loop graviton 2-point function has a UV divergence outside the original two-derivative counterterm family;
2. absorbing it requires a new higher-derivative BRST-invariant operator;
3. the paper’s own subtraction replaces a constant (Z_H) by a momentum-dependent one;
4. the TEGR reduction is therefore not stable under the claimed quantum renormalization.

Hence this theory is **not one-loop renormalizable in the standard BPHZ/algebraic-renormalizati

on sense**, and not “in the same manner as Yang–Mills on Minkowski space.” ([arXiv][1])
