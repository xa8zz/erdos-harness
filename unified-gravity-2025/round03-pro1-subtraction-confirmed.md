I checked the current primary source directly: Partanen & Tulkki, **“Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model”**, arXiv:2310.01460v11 (6 Aug 2025), later published in *Rep. Prog. Phys.* **88** (2025) 057802, DOI 10.1088/1361-6633/adc82e. My verdict is that your reading is **basically right in substance**, but with two important corrections: the paper’s later scalar (\Xi_{1L}) is **not** the 1PI tensor self-energy, and the printed (\delta Z_H^{(1)}) formula does **not** itself contain (\log p^2). Even with those corrections, the gravitational-sector renormalization in §9.8 does **not** fit standard Yang–Mills/BPHZ renormalizability of the original local bare action. ([arXiv][1])

1. The relevant places are exactly where you expected. The paper’s renormalization-factor setup is in **§9.3–9.4** (printed pp. 35–36), especially eqs. **(157)**, **(160)**, **(161)**, **(162)**. The graviton self-energy calculation and subtraction are in **§9.8** (printed pp. 39–40), especially eqs. **(187)–(205)**, with the projector definitions in **(190)–(195)** and the key propagator-level redefinition in **(196)**. ([arXiv][2])

2. **Claim (1): confirmed in substance, with a notation caveat.** The true 1PI graviton two-point tensor is given in eqs. **(187)** and **(188)**, and both have an overall (\kappa p^4) prefactor:
   [
   i\Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}*{1L,\text{electron}}
   = \frac{i\kappa c,p^4}{480\pi^2\hbar}{\cdots + [1/\epsilon*{UV}+\log(4\pi\mu^2e^{-\gamma}/m_e^2c^2)],\hat P_B^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda} + \cdots},
   ]
   [
   i\Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}*{1L,\text{photon}}
   = \frac{i\kappa c,p^4}{240\pi^2\hbar}{\hat P*{81,94,30}^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda} + [1/\epsilon_{UV}+\log(-4\pi\mu^2e^{-\gamma}/p^2)],\hat P_{3,2,1}^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}}.
   ]
   So the 1PI divergence is degree 4 in momentum, not Yang–Mills-like degree 2. The caveat is that after eq. **(196)** the paper reuses (\Xi_{1L}) for a **scalar propagator correction factor** obtained after sandwiching the 1PI tensor between two tree propagators; that scalar factor scales like (\kappa p^2) in eqs. **(198)** and **(199)**. So if one literally calls the later scalar (\Xi_{1L}) “the 1PI self-energy,” that is a misreading; the 1PI object is the indexed tensor in eqs. **(187)–(188)**. ([arXiv][2])

3. **Claim (2): confirmed.** The bare-field relation is introduced in the ordinary way in eq. **(157)**,
   [
   H^{\mu\nu}*{\text{bare}}=\sqrt{Z_H},H^{\mu\nu},
   ]
   with the generic loop expansion (Z_i=1+\lambda \delta Z_i^{(1)}+\lambda^2\delta Z_i^{(2)}+\cdots) in eq. **(161)**. In the renormalized Lagrangian, the gravitational quadratic term is written in eq. **(162)** as
   [
   L*{g,\text{kin}}=\frac1{4\kappa}H_{\rho\mu\nu}S^{\rho\mu\nu}+(Z_H-1)\frac1{4\kappa}H_{\rho\mu\nu}S^{\rho\mu\nu},
   ]
   and the corresponding 1PI counterterm vertex in §9.8 is eq. **(189)**,
   [
   i\Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}*{1L,CT}= i(Z_H-1)p^2\hat P^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}*{1,2,1}.
   ]
   No independent higher-derivative quadratic graviton counterterm is displayed in the bare action. At the level of §9.3–9.4, (Z_H) is plainly being introduced as an ordinary renormalization factor, not as a kinematic form factor. ([arXiv][2])

4. **Claim (3): confirmed in substance, with a small correction.** The subtraction conditions are exactly eq. **(203)**:
   [
   \Xi_{1L}\big|*{p^2=0}=0,\qquad \left.\frac{\Xi*{1L}}{p^4}\right|*{p^2\to\infty}=0.
   ]
   The paper says these are imposed so the Newtonian-potential corrections are integrable and to forbid arbitrary added analytic terms in (p^2) or higher powers. Then eqs. **(204)**–**(205)** give (\delta Z*{H,\text{electron}}^{(1)}) and (\delta Z_{H,\text{photon}}^{(1)}) as explicit **momentum-dependent** functions. Eq. **(204)** visibly depends on (p^2), inverse powers of (p^2), (m_e^2), and logs; eq. **(205)** is proportional to (p^2). So the paper really does resolve the mismatch by making (\delta Z_H^{(1)}) momentum dependent. The small correction is that the displayed (\delta Z_H) formulas do **not** themselves contain (\log p^2); the (\log(-p^2)) term appears later in the renormalized photon contribution, eq. **(207)**, not inside (\delta Z_H). Also, the printed eq. **(204)** contains a somewhat odd term that looks like (16m_e^2c^2/p^4); whether typo or not, the key point is unchanged: (\delta Z_H^{(1)}) is not a constant. ([arXiv][2])

5. On the interpretive question, the **renormalization conditions themselves are not the issue**. Algebraic renormalization allows one to impose conditions at special kinematic points, including **zero momentum, on-shell momentum, or large external momenta**. Finite renormalizations can move between such schemes. So a condition like eq. **(203)** is not, by itself, outside BPHZ. ([CERN Document Server][3])

6. What standard BPHZ/algebraic renormalization does require is **locality of the 1PI counterterms**. In BPHZ, primitively divergent graphs are subtracted by a **Taylor polynomial in the external momenta**; those counterterms are polynomials in external momenta and are interpreted as wave-function, mass, and coupling counterterms in the Lagrangian. In the algebraic-renormalization language, the QAP guarantees the **locality** and **polynomial character** of the counterterms, and the relevant 1PI breakings/counterterms are integrated local polynomials. Connes–Kreimer’s Hopf-algebra reformulation is likewise framed for theories renormalizable by **local counterterms**. ([INSPIRE][4])

7. Under that standard criterion, the paper’s displayed graviton 1PI divergence is **not** absorbed by the original (Z_H) counterterm basis. From eqs. **(187)** and **(194)**, the divergent part of the electron loop is
   [
   i\Xi^{\text{div}}*{1L,\text{electron}}
   \propto \frac{i\kappa c}{\epsilon*{UV}}
   \Big(p^4\hat P_{3,2,1}-10m_e^2c^2,p^2\hat P_{1,2,1}-30m_e^4c^4,P_{1,0,1}\Big),
   ]
   while eq. **(188)** gives a photon-loop pole proportional to (p^4\hat P_{3,2,1}). But the only displayed quadratic gravitational counterterm is eq. **(189)**,
   [
   i(Z_H-1)p^2\hat P_{1,2,1}.
   ]
   So there is not only a **degree mismatch** ((p^4) vs (p^2)); there is also a **tensor-structure mismatch** ((\hat P_{3,2,1}) and (P_{1,0,1}) appear in the divergence, while only (\hat P_{1,2,1}) appears in the bare counterterm). In standard BPHZ terms, the natural interpretation of the 1PI pole is therefore: the local counterterm basis must be **enlarged**, at minimum by higher-derivative quadratic gravitational operators, and in fact by more than a single field-strength renormalization. Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux make exactly this distinction in general: once higher-mass-dimension interactions are needed, one has left ordinary power-counting renormalizability and entered an EFT-type setting. ([arXiv][2])

8. That means your interpretive claim (4) is **right in spirit but slightly too narrow in form**. The standard BPHZ reading is not merely “one new (H' \Box H') counterterm and done.” Rather, the paper’s 1PI pole terms indicate a need for an **enlarged local quadratic operator basis**, including higher-derivative structures and additional tensor structures not present in eq. **(162)**. Because the paper defines (H_a{}_\nu) as dimensionless in its unscaled convention, the dimensional bookkeeping is disguised; after the usual canonical normalization, the (p^4) pole is exactly the sort of four-derivative / higher-dimension quadratic term that is not part of an ordinary Yang–Mills two-derivative bare action. ([arXiv][2])

9. The paper, however, does **not** actually do that 1PI local-counterterm analysis. Instead, it goes to eq. **(196)**, projects the indexed 1PI tensor into the scalar propagator correction factor (\Xi_{1L}), and then chooses a **momentum-dependent** (\delta Z_H^{(1)}(p^2)) from eqs. **(203)**–**(205)**. Read literally as a renormalization factor in eqs. **(157)** and **(162)**, that is no longer an ordinary field renormalization constant; it is a kinematic form factor. Its full dependence on inverse powers of (p^2) is nonlocal if interpreted as part of the bare action. So there is no “third option” here that preserves standard BPHZ renormalizability of the original parameter set. The two standard readings are: either enlarge the local action (EFT-style), or admit that the paper is using a nonlocal/projected subtraction at the propagator level rather than a local 1PI counterterm subtraction. ([arXiv][2])

10. The main explicit **misreading** I would correct is this: the later scalar (\Xi_{1L}) in eqs. **(196)**–**(199)** is **not** the same object as the indexed 1PI self-energy in eqs. **(187)**–**(189)**. The paper’s own notation encourages confusion because both are written with (\Xi_{1L}). Once that is separated, the situation becomes clear: the 1PI tensor divergence is (O(\kappa p^4)), while the projected propagator factor is (O(\kappa p^2)). A second, smaller correction is that the paper’s displayed (\delta Z_H^{(1)}) formula does not literally contain (\log p^2); that logarithm appears in the renormalized remainder, not in the “renormalization factor” itself. ([arXiv][2])

**Final verdict:** as actually written in §9.8, the paper’s one-loop gravitational-sector renormalization does **not** fit the standard BPHZ/Yang–Mills framework for renormalizability of the original local bare action. The paper does show that, after projecting to a scalar propagator correction factor, one can choose momentum-dependent subtractions satisfying certain physical conditions. But that is **not** the same as showing that the 1PI graviton two-point divergences are absorbed by the original finite set of local Yang–Mills-style counterterms. On a standard BPHZ/algebraic-renormalization reading, the displayed 1PI divergence would require an enlarged local higher-derivative counterterm basis; taken literally as written, the paper instead uses a momentum-dependent propagator-level subtraction. So the paper does **not** establish the claimed Yang–Mills-style one-loop renormalizability of the gravitational sector. ([arXiv][2])
