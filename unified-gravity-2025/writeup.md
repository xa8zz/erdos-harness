## TL;DR

Partanen and Tulkki's 2025 paper in *Reports on Progress in Physics* claims a one-loop renormalizable quantum theory of gravity unified with the Standard Model, organized as a $U(1)^4$ abelian gauge theory of a "space-time dimension field" with a teleparallel reduction. After reading the published version (arXiv:2310.01460v11 / Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 057802) and the six author follow-ups against the standard renormalization, BRST cohomology, and teleparallel literature, I do not think the strict headline claim survives. Four independent structural obstructions block it. The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 quietly concedes the most important of them and exposes a branch fork in the program's identity that the original paper blurred. Four coherent descendants exist, each abandoning at least one stated goal. I lay out the obstructions, the fork, and the descendant taxonomy below, with paper-internal equation citations and verbatim quotes where load-bearing.

This is not a takedown. Partanen and Tulkki have written a serious paper, it survived peer review at a respected journal, and the program has generated specific falsifiable classical predictions (notably a 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection signature). What it has not produced is a renormalizable quantum field theory of gravity in the BPHZ sense. The honest framing is that the strict claim fails, the program has coherent paths forward, and the public record needs an explicit branch choice.

---

## 1. The paper and what it claims

Partanen and Tulkki, *Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model*, Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 057802 (2025); arXiv:2310.01460; DOI 10.1088/1361-6633/adc82e. The arXiv version went through eleven revisions between October 2023 and August 2025, with v11 about 270 times the size of v1. Most of the growth happened during 2025 peer review. Two corrigenda have been published: Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 069501 (May 2025), correcting a factor of two in the projector tensor in equations 47, 109, 120, 142, and 162; and Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 079501 (July 2025), correcting a single factor of $1/2$ in equation 122 with no downstream effect.

The construction takes an eight-spinor representation of the Lagrangian and introduces a "space-time dimension field." Its four $U(1)$ symmetries are gauged. The resulting theory contains four gauge potentials $H_{a\mu}$ with abelian field strengths $H_{a\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu H_{a\nu} - \partial_\nu H_{a\mu}$ (paper eqs. 39, 55–57). The gravity kinetic Lagrangian is a teleparallel-superpotential contraction,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa}\, H_{a\mu\nu} S^{a\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g},
$$
(eq. 61), where $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is the soldered superpotential built from $H$ and the tetrad. The paper warns explicitly that $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is not the field strength itself in a soldered gauge theory; this distinguishes the action from an abelian Pontryagin density. (The distinction matters; I will come back to it.) The graviton propagator in harmonic gauge is the standard linearized-GR result (eq. 138).

The three stated goals (paper section 9 and abstract):

1. **Perturbative renormalizability** at one loop, organized "in the same way as the gauge theories of the Standard Model."
2. **Classical reduction to GR** via the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (TEGR) in the Weitzenböck gauge, obtained from unified gravity by a gravity-gauge-field-dependent geometric condition.
3. **Equivalence principle** via equality of renormalized inertial and gravitational masses.

The paper has received uniformly positive popular-science coverage (zmescience, sciencealert, sciencedaily, Aalto press releases). The technical literature has been quieter. Will Barker posted a critical analysis one month after v1 (arXiv:2311.11790, November 2023). Farrukh Chishtie published a critique in *Chinese Journal of Physics* in May 2026 framed as "nine interconnected structural limitations" (DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001). The text is paywall-blocked and I have not been able to read it; I can confirm only its existence and abstract-level thrust. Bouali Bensouilah published a supportive tree-level calculation in *Canadian Journal of Physics* in 2026 (DOI 10.1139/cjp-2025-0362).

This writeup is a structural analysis of the renormalizability claim and its coherence. I do not dispute the classical existence of the construction. I dispute that the strict version of goal (1) holds and that the original presentation of goals (2) and (3) is coherent in light of the 2025 follow-ups.

## 2. Four obstructions to the strict one-loop renormalizability claim

I read the paper's section 9 (renormalization) carefully against the standard apparatus: BPHZ subtraction, local BRST cohomology in the sense of Barnich, Brandt, and Henneaux, and the teleparallel one-loop literature. Four independent obstructions block the claim. Each builds on classical results; the contribution here is their combination as applied to this specific construction.

### Obstruction A: matter-loop Weyl-squared and axial-current-quartic counterterms

This is the textbook problem of matter-coupled gravity, repackaged in the paper's variables. A Dirac-fermion loop in a gravity background generates a Weyl-squared counterterm,
$$
\Delta \Gamma^{(1)}_{\text{div}} \supset \frac{b}{(4\pi)^2 \varepsilon} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
$$
with Duff coefficient $b = 1/20$ per Dirac fermion (Duff 1994). Under the paper's weak-field map $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{\kappa}\, h_{\mu\nu}[A]$, this pulls back to a four-derivative $(\partial F)^2$ operator of mass dimension 6 in the $U(1)^4$ gauge variables, not present in the bare $F^2$-type kinetic term. The Einstein–Dirac one-loop calculation additionally produces an irreducible axial-current quartic, $(\bar\psi \gamma^a \gamma_5 \psi)^4$, a dimension-12 eight-spinor operator (Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974, *Phys. Rev. D* **10**, 411). The axial current has dimension 3, so $(J_5)^4$ is genuinely dimension 12 before any factors of $\kappa$. The terminology "four-fermion" sometimes used for this operator is loose; it is more precisely an eight-spinor object.

Both counterterms are local, Lorentz invariant, gauge invariant, and BRST-closed at ghost number 0. By the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux classification (*Phys. Rep.* **338**, 439, 2000; arXiv:hep-th/0002245), they sit squarely in the admissible counterterm cohomology. BRST does not forbid them; it allows them. In an abelian theory with abelian gauge fixing, Slavnov–Taylor identities collapse to Ward identities, which constrain divergences to be gauge invariant but do not collapse independent gauge-invariant scalars into a single one. The bare action contains neither operator. No multiplicative $Z$-factor in the original parameter set can absorb a $p^4$ inverse propagator structure into a $p^2$ one, and no multiplicative $Z$ can absorb a dimension-12 operator into a dimension-4 mass term.

This obstruction is independent of unified gravity specifically. It applies to any two-derivative gravity theory coupled to fermionic matter in 4D, and has been known for fifty years. The paper does not address it; the renormalization analysis in section 9 restricts itself to the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational fields and leaves the broader Standard Model matter sector for future work. I will return to the narrowness of the demonstrated result in section 4.

### Obstruction B: paper-internal counterterm-basis mismatch

This is the decisive paper-internal critique. It does not require importing external results; the obstruction is visible in the paper's own one-loop formulas. I read section 9.8 (graviton self-energy) carefully against the bare counterterm structure displayed in section 9.3–9.4. The mismatch is sharp.

The paper writes the bare field relation in eq. 157 as $H^{\mu\nu}_{\text{bare}} = \sqrt{Z_H}\, H^{\mu\nu}$, with the standard loop expansion $Z_H = 1 + \lambda \delta Z_H^{(1)} + \ldots$ (eq. 161). The renormalized gravity kinetic Lagrangian (eq. 162) is
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu} + (Z_H - 1) \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu},
$$
giving a counterterm vertex (eq. 189) of the form $i (Z_H - 1) p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}$. So the available bare quadratic counterterm has degree 2 in momentum and projector structure $\hat P_{1,2,1}$.

The one-loop graviton self-energy from the electron and photon loops (eqs. 187–188) has overall scaling $\kappa p^4 / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$:
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{electron}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{480 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log\frac{4\pi \mu^2 e^{-\gamma}}{m_e^2 c^2}\right] \hat P_B + \ldots,
$$
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{photon}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{240 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log(\ldots)\right] \hat P_{3,2,1} + \ldots
$$
where $\hat P_B = \hat P_{3,2,1} - 10 m_e^2 c^2 p^{-2} \hat P_{1,2,1} - 30 m_e^4 c^4 p^{-4} P_{1,0,1}$ (eq. 194). So the divergence sits in projectors $\hat P_{3,2,1}$ and $P_{1,0,1}$ as well as $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, and scales as $p^4$.

A constant $Z_H$ times a $p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}$ counterterm cannot absorb a $p^4 \hat P_{3,2,1}$ divergence. There is both a derivative-degree mismatch and a tensor-structure mismatch. The paper's resolution is in eqs. 203–205: impose the subtraction conditions
$$
\Xi_{1L} \Big|_{p^2 = 0} = 0, \qquad \left(\frac{\Xi_{1L}}{p^4}\right)\Big|_{p^2 \to \infty} = 0,
$$
and let $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ become explicitly momentum dependent, with functional form involving $p^2$, $m_e^2/p^2$, $m_e^4/p^4$, and logarithms. (A small notational caveat: the symbol $\Xi_{1L}$ in eq. 196 onward refers to a scalar propagator correction factor obtained by sandwiching the 1PI tensor between two tree propagators; this scalar object scales as $\kappa p^2$ and is distinct from the indexed 1PI tensor in eqs. 187–188. The displayed $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ formulas do not literally contain $\log p^2$; that logarithm appears in the renormalized remainder at eq. 207. Neither notational issue affects the substantive obstruction.)

In standard BPHZ and algebraic renormalization, the natural readings of this prescription are:

(i) Local enlargement of the counterterm basis with new higher-derivative gauge-invariant operators (schematically $H'_{a\mu\nu} \Box H'^{a\mu\nu}$), which is exactly what an EFT does. But then power-counting renormalizability has been abandoned.

(ii) Nonlocal subtraction at the propagator level, outside the BPHZ class. This is not what the standard "renormalizable like Yang–Mills" framing means.

The paper does neither. It uses momentum-dependent propagator-level subtraction while presenting it as multiplicative field renormalization. Read literally as a renormalization of the original local bare action, it is not Standard-Model-style BPHZ closure. This is the sharpest paper-internal problem and the one I would invite Partanen and Tulkki to address first.

### Obstruction C: teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR one-parameter family

The paper's goal (2), exact classical reduction to TEGR via a geometric condition $\eta = \eta(A)$, adds an additional constraint: the reduction must hold off-shell at the quantum level, or it is not a stable identification. Casadio, Kuntz, and Paci (*Eur. Phys. J. C* **82**, 186, 2022; arXiv:2110.04325) settled what happens. Quantum matter loops on a teleparallel background generate one-loop divergences whose structure depends on the coupling prescription. Under full equivalence-principle coupling (translation plus local Lorentz), the divergences are quartic in torsion: the teleparallel image of $R^2$-type curvature-squared counterterms in metric variables. Under gauge-only translation coupling, the divergences are quadratic in torsion in the Yang–Mills contraction $T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu}$ alone, not in the specific linear combination
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{1}{4} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\nu\mu\rho} - T_\rho{}^{\rho\mu} T^\nu{}_{\nu\mu}
$$
that defines the TEGR torsion scalar (Aldrovandi–Pereira 2013; Maluf 2013, eqs. 23–27).

TEGR has one gravitational coupling, Newton's constant. A finite parameter redefinition cannot absorb an independent $T^2$ contraction or a $T^4$ term. Absorption requires enlargement to a three-parameter "New GR" action with independent coefficients $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ for the three torsion-quadratic invariants. That enlargement violates the off-shell closure of the $\eta = \eta(A)$ reduction.

This is independent of obstruction B. Even before quantizing the gravitational field itself, matter loops on a TEGR background fail to close into the TEGR action. The classical $\mathcal{T} = R$ (up to a boundary term) identity does not survive renormalization because it requires the specific 1:2:-1 ratio of coefficients, and that ratio is not radiatively stable.

### Obstruction D: Barker's spectrum analysis

Will Barker (Cambridge, now Czech Academy of Sciences) posted arXiv:2311.11790 one month after Partanen and Tulkki's v1. The abstract reports a "ghostly teleparallel vacuum" in the weak-field zero-coupling limit. The paper body, which I read carefully because the abstract summary is too compressed to act on, sharpens this into specific spin-mode structure.

In the symmetric interpretation of the unified-gravity weak-field zero-coupling limit, the two healthy graviton polarizations require one sign of $\kappa$ while five additional massless polarizations require the opposite sign of $\kappa$. Ghosts are unavoidable; no consistent choice of $\kappa$ makes all seven polarizations healthy simultaneously. In the asymmetric / teleparallel interpretation, Barker reports six ghost polarizations. In a bimetric reading of the same field content, the spectrum is still not unitary. He suggests bimetric, vacuum-expectation-value, or finite-coupling extensions as possible escape routes but does not establish that any of them work.

This is not a rigorous no-go theorem for every possible finite-coupling completion. It is a free-field-limit spectral analysis, and finite-coupling effects can in principle change the propagating mode content. But it is a sharp warning: even before renormalization is attempted, the linearized theory does not obviously contain only healthy helicity-2 gravity plus gauge redundancy. The Partanen–Tulkki program needs an explicit unitary-spectrum demonstration in the physical branch. None has been published.

Barker's later work (arXiv:2406.12826 on Poincaré gauge theories; arXiv:2505.23894 on metric-affine gravity; arXiv:2506.02111 on parity-violating spectra; arXiv:2510.08201 on pure $R^2$ gravity) develops the same spectral-analysis machinery on related theories but does not re-engage Partanen and Tulkki by name. As far as I can find, Barker's 2023 paper is the only credentialed third-party arXiv critique of unified gravity that has remained in the public discourse for two years without rebuttal.

## 3. Why the four obstructions cannot be addressed simultaneously inside the strict family

The obstructions are not four independent annoyances. They are the same counterterm-basis-closure failure expressed in four different languages.

A says: matter loops generate spin-2 stress-tensor curvature-squared and axial-current-quartic counterterms not in the bare action.

B says: the paper's own one-loop self-energy has $p^4$ tensor structure not in the bare $p^2$ counterterm.

C says: TEGR's one-parameter family is not closed under matter renormalization.

D says: the free-theory spectrum at the relevant decoupling limit contains opposite-sign-$\kappa$ ghost modes.

A, B, and C reduce to the same statement: the bare action's parameter set is too small. D says: even before we worry about the bare action's parameters, the propagating modes are not all healthy.

The strict family (local $U(1)^4$ gauge theory, finite bare coupling set, exact off-shell closure of the TEGR reduction, two-derivative gravitational dynamics, healthy spectrum) cannot absorb all four simultaneously. The argument is structural: by the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux classification, every ghost-number-0 BRST cocycle that actually occurs must already have an independent coupling in the bare action (stability of the action under renormalization). The divergent cocycles include $C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^2$, $R^2$, the torsion-quadratic invariants beyond $\mathcal{T}$, the torsion-quartic invariants, and the axial-current quartic. The original bare action contains none of them. Adding them enlarges the family. Refusing to add them leaves the divergences uncanceled.

This is the impossibility statement: no local-BPHZ-renormalizable descendant within the strict original family can absorb A, B, C, D simultaneously while preserving all three stated goals at original strength. At least one of (exact TEGR closure, two-derivative gravitational dynamics, finite-coupling renormalizability, healthy free spectrum, $U(1)^4$ field-space organization) must be abandoned.

I want to be honest about the standing of this statement. It is not a theorem in the formal sense. It is a structural inference from (i) the explicit divergent-cocycle list in the standard 4D matter-coupled-gravity literature, (ii) the cohomological classification, and (iii) the paper-internal counterterm basis. A counterexample would require either showing that some of the "divergent" cocycles actually vanish in this construction (which would need a new symmetry or cancellation theorem not present in the published record), or showing that one of the four obstructions misreads the construction. I have looked for both. Three rounds of independent GPT Pro dispatches in the research program behind this writeup, thirteen attempts in total across R02, R03, R04, R06, R07, agreed on the structural verdict via different framings and emphases. That is suggestive of within-family attractor convergence but does not rise to cross-family verification. I would welcome an attempt at refutation.

## 4. The branch fork: what the 2025 follow-ups quietly conceded

The most consequential new finding in this writeup did not come from re-reading the original paper. It came from reading the July 2025 follow-up, arXiv:2507.07790, "Extending unified gravity to account for graviton-graviton interaction." The follow-up exposes a structural fork in the program's identity that the original paper papered over.

Three direct quotes from arXiv:2507.07790 (verified against the html v2 of the preprint):

> There is no equivalence transformation from unified gravity to TEGR.

> The substitutions that produce TEGR break the gauge symmetry of unified gravity.

> The new gauge-invariant graviton-graviton self-interaction term does not contribute to the TEGR-reduced Lagrangian.

And on renormalization, the same paper closes with:

> Regarding the Feynman diagrams of UG, our extension introduces the triple-graviton vertex, whose implications to the renormalization of UG are left as a topic of further work.

These four sentences are very important. They mean that the program is not one theory with two equivalent presentations. It is two structurally distinct theories connected by a symmetry-breaking substitution:

**The Minkowski-UG branch** preserves the four $U(1)$ gauge symmetry, contains the new triple-graviton vertex introduced in 2507.07790, and carries the 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection deviations predicted in arXiv:2505.14446. Its renormalization properties are unknown; the authors explicitly defer them. Its tree-level phenomenology is genuinely non-GR.

**The TEGR-reduced branch** is recovered from the Minkowski-UG branch by substitutions that break the gauge symmetry. It does not contain the new graviton-graviton self-interaction term in the same form (the extension term vanishes under the TEGR reduction, per the third quote above). It classically agrees with GR.

The original paper's framing ("we have a theory that reproduces GR via the teleparallel equivalence and also predicts new effects") is incoherent as a unified statement once these two branches are distinguished. The 23.3% / 26.7% deviations belong to the Minkowski-UG branch specifically. They are not predictions of a theory that classically reduces to GR. They are predictions of a separate theory that is connected to GR only through a substitution that breaks its own gauge symmetry.

The branch fork also reframes the renormalization question. The original section 9 analysis was carried out in the UGM/Minkowski formulation, on a theory without graviton self-interaction. The 2025 follow-up admits that graviton self-interaction is a necessary extension of the Minkowski theory; quoting the same paper, "the new gauge-invariant graviton-graviton self-interaction term" is required for problems involving interactions between gravitational fields. So the theory whose one-loop renormalization was analyzed in 2310.01460 is not the theory the authors now regard as physically necessary. The renormalization analysis must be restarted on the extended theory. The 2025 follow-up acknowledges this and defers it.

That is a substantial concession. It means the original headline claim, even setting aside obstructions A through D, was renormalization of a sector of an incomplete theory: the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational fields in the Minkowski formulation without graviton self-interaction. The paper's section 9 itself flags the restriction; it does not claim renormalization of the full Standard Model coupled to gravity. The popular-press framing, that unified gravity solves the problem of quantum gravity unified with the Standard Model, overstates what the paper actually demonstrates.

## 5. Four coherent descendants

The strict claim does not survive, but the program is not empty. Four structurally distinct descendants remain coherent, each abandoning at least one original goal. This refines the two-descendant taxonomy that emerged in R04 of the research program; reading Barker's body content in R07 split the descendants further into four.

### 5.1 Teleparallel / tetrad effective field theory

The most conservative descendant. Take the bare action to contain the full local operator basis allowed by symmetries: torsion-quadratic terms with independent coefficients ($c_1, c_2, c_3$), torsion-quartic terms, curvature-squared terms ($\alpha_C C^2 + \alpha_R R^2 + \alpha_G G$), and the higher-dimensional fermionic operator tower including the axial-current quartic $(J_5^\mu J_{5\mu})^2$. Match the leading torsion-quadratic coefficients to the TEGR values $(c_1, c_2, c_3) = (1/4, 1/2, -1)$ at low energies. Treat higher-derivative terms as perturbative insertions, not resummed propagator poles.

This is Donoghue–Burgess EFT of gravity (Donoghue 1994, *Phys. Rev. D* **50**, 3874; Burgess 2004 *Living Reviews* **7**, 5) with a teleparallel parameterization. It preserves the classical GR/TEGR limit and a low-energy form of the equivalence principle (universal coupling to the stress-energy tensor below the cutoff). It abandons strict perturbative renormalizability in the Yang–Mills sense; the theory is predictive order by order in $E/\Lambda_*$ rather than to all orders with a finite parameter set.

This is the closest descendant to the original spirit of the construction. Chishtie's USMEG-EFT framework (arXiv:2503.09796, *Canadian J. Phys.* accepted) is structurally aligned with this descendant, reached by different machinery (Lagrange-multiplier-restricted GR plus Appelquist–Carazzone decoupling rather than teleparallel counterterm enlargement). The fact that an independent author reaches an EFT-based unification verdict via different machinery is corroborating signal for this exit route.

### 5.2 New-GR teleparallel renormalization

A narrower descendant that addresses obstruction C alone. Promote the three torsion-quadratic coefficients to independent renormalized parameters but stop short of including the full higher-derivative tower. This is Casadio–Kuntz–Paci's "easy fix" at one loop. It abandons exact TEGR closure (the 1:2:-1 ratio is no longer protected) but keeps teleparallel structure.

The descendant has known structural risks. Beltrán Jiménez, Heisenberg, and Koivisto (*Phys. Lett. B* **805**, 135422, 2020) showed that generic teleparallel quadratic theories around Minkowski typically contain extra symmetric rank-2 fields plus a 2-form, and that the linear symmetries needed for viability can fail nonlinearly, producing strong-coupling or pathological behavior. New GR is a good leading EFT basis. It is not an established UV completion. A separate spectrum and stability check is required before this descendant can claim more than the EFT descendant in section 5.1.

### 5.3 Higher-derivative curvature gravity with fakeons

The descendant that preserves perturbative renormalizability most strongly. Take the bare action in Stelle / Fradkin–Tseytlin form,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{QG}} = \sqrt{-g}\left[-\Lambda - \frac{\bar M_P^2}{2} R + \alpha (R_{\mu\nu} R^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{3} R^2) - \frac{\xi}{6} R^2 \right] + \mathcal{L}_{\text{matter}}.
$$
Stelle (1977, *Phys. Rev. D* **16**, 953) proved renormalizability. Fradkin and Tseytlin (1982, *Nucl. Phys. B* **201**, 469) proved asymptotic freedom of the essential couplings. The $1/p^4$ UV behavior of the graviton propagator makes the theory power-counting renormalizable with general renormalizable matter sectors.

The standard problem is a massive spin-2 ghost in the propagator. Anselmi and Piva (2018, arXiv:1803.07777) showed that the fakeon prescription quantizes the massive spin-2 pole as a fakeon rather than a physical ghost, preserving perturbative unitarity at the cost of modified microcausality above the fakeon scale. The fakeon prescription does not affect renormalization (it acts at the propagator-pole level only); the beta functions and one-loop counterterms are computed explicitly.

This descendant abandons exact TEGR closure entirely; GR survives only as an IR / classical limit, not as the UV theory. It abandons the "equal renormalized inertial and gravitational masses" mechanism in favor of standard diffeomorphism-invariant universal coupling. It abandons standard microcausality above the fakeon scale. What it gains is the strongest available version of goal (1): gravity on the same perturbative footing as renormalizable matter sectors.

### 5.4 Minkowski-UG as a distinct gauge theory

The descendant the 2025 follow-ups appear to be moving toward, without yet stating explicitly. Drop the rhetoric of GR equivalence. Treat the gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch as a distinct classical and quantum theory of gravity on its own merits. The right benchmarks are then not "does it reproduce GR" but the standard local-QFT health checks applied to this specific theory:

- Healthy particle spectrum around the relevant vacuum (addressing Barker).
- Local counterterm basis closure for the full theory including the triple-graviton vertex (addressing B and the 2025 deferral).
- Viable Ward / Slavnov–Taylor identities.
- Consistent EFT expansion.
- Experimentally viable 2PN polarization predictions (addressing 2505.14446's 23.3% / 26.7% signature).

Bensouilah's 2026 calculation of tree-level photon-photon scattering in UGM, reproducing the classical Tolman–Ehrenfest–Podolsky description of the gravitational field produced by light, is a useful consistency check inside this descendant. The atomic-redshift, perihelion-precession, and light-deflection follow-ups by Partanen, Tulkki, and collaborators (arXiv:2505.14446, 2506.14447, 2506.22057) are also natural inhabitants of this descendant.

This is an open research program, not a completed quantum gravity solution. The 2PN polarization signature is genuinely non-GR. In classical metric GR plus Maxwell theory, ray-optics light propagation is polarization-blind; the known polarization-dependent gravitational effects (spin-optics, helicity corrections, and Drummond–Hathrell QED vacuum polarization in curved space) are all sub-leading to ray optics, not 23% effects at second post-Newtonian order. It is testable via LATOR-class missions. Whether the underlying theory survives the local-QFT health checks is open.

## 6. The surrounding discourse

A few notes on where this writeup sits relative to the existing literature.

**The author trajectory.** Partanen and Tulkki have written six tree-level follow-ups plus two corrigenda: the eight-spinor QED companion paper (PRA 109, 032224, 2024), light deflection (2505.14446), perihelion precession (2506.14447), atomic Dirac redshift (2506.22057), the graviton-graviton extension (2507.07790), and the corrigenda Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 069501 and 88, 079501. All follow-ups stay at tree level. The July 2025 paper explicitly defers loop renormalization analysis of the extended theory. The trajectory is consistent with a program moving toward descendant 5.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct theory) without yet making the framing explicit.

**Independent technical critique.** Two credentialed third-party critiques exist in the public record. Barker (arXiv:2311.11790, November 2023) is the ghost-spectrum analysis I cover in obstruction D. Chishtie (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001) is framed as "nine interconnected structural limitations." I have not been able to access the full text; ScienceDirect returns access restrictions and ResearchGate has no preprint. I can confirm the paper's bibliographic existence and abstract-level thrust but cannot independently evaluate the nine specific claims. Chishtie's separate USMEG-EFT framework paper (arXiv:2503.09796) reaches an EFT-based unification verdict structurally aligned with descendant 5.1 via independent machinery, which I count as corroboration of that exit route.

**Supportive tree-level work.** Bensouilah (*Canadian J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1139/cjp-2025-0362) computes tree-level photon-photon scattering in UGM and finds it reproduces the classical Tolman–Ehrenfest–Podolsky description. This is a positive consistency check inside descendant 5.4. It does not touch obstructions A through D.

**Where this writeup goes beyond the public record.** As far as I can find, the paper-internal subtraction defect documented in obstruction B is not in the public discourse. The chain from $\kappa p^4 / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$ 1PI pole through constant-$Z_H$ bare counterterm to nonpolynomial $\delta Z_H^{(1)}(p^2)$ momentum-dependent subtraction, with paper-equation citations 157, 161, 162, 187–189, 196, 203–205, is not stated anywhere I can locate. Casadio–Kuntz–Paci is at a higher level of generality (teleparallel matter loops in general, not Partanen–Tulkki's specific subtraction prescription). Barker addresses spectrum, not counterterm-basis structure. The four-obstruction combination, the structural-impossibility framing, the explicit branch-fork articulation, and the four-descendant taxonomy are also, as far as I can find, not in the public record. None of the individual pieces is original; their combination as applied here appears to be.

**Where the public record is ahead of this writeup.** Barker's spectrum analysis sharpens what I would otherwise have stated only at the abstract level. Chishtie's nine structural limitations may overlap with or extend the obstructions I cover; I cannot say without reading the paper. The author follow-up trajectory makes the branch fork visible in the public record in a way that the original paper did not.

**Popular press versus technical record.** The popular-science coverage of unified gravity has been uniformly positive and substantially overstates the technical status. zmescience, sciencealert, sciencedaily, Phys.org, Earth.com, the Aalto press release, and others present the paper as having solved or substantially advanced quantum gravity unified with the Standard Model. The technical record does not support that framing. The paper's own renormalization claim is restricted to a sector of an incomplete theory, the strict version of that claim does not survive structural analysis, and the 2025 follow-ups have moved the program into a fork the original paper did not articulate. I do not say this to disparage the paper or the authors; the popular-press dynamics around quantum gravity papers are not their responsibility. But the gap between the popular framing and the technical status is large enough to be worth naming.

## 7. What is settled, what is open, what would change my mind

**Settled (in my view, after the analysis above):**

- The strict one-loop renormalizability claim in the original family is not defensible. Obstructions A, B, C, D are independent failures of the same closure condition.
- The original section 9 analysis covers only the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational sector, not the full Standard Model coupled to gravity.
- The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 exposes a branch fork between the gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch and the TEGR-reduced branch. They are not equivalent presentations of one theory.
- The 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection signature is a prediction of the Minkowski-UG branch specifically, not of "unified gravity" as a unified concept.
- Four coherent descendants exist (sections 5.1–5.4), each abandoning at least one stated goal.

**Open:**

- The full content of Chishtie's "nine interconnected structural limitations" (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026). I cannot evaluate this without access to the paper.
- Cross-family verification (Gemini DeepThink, Claude with extended thinking) of the within-Pro convergence behind this writeup. Thirteen GPT Pro dispatches reached the same structural verdict via different framings; this is suggestive but not conclusive.
- The full loop renormalization analysis of the extended Minkowski-UG theory with the triple-graviton vertex from 2507.07790. The authors defer it.
- The future-experimental status of the 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection prediction. LATOR-class missions could test it.
- Whether descendant 5.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct theory) survives the standard local-QFT health checks. This is the open research question.

**What would change my mind on the strict claim:**

- A demonstration that obstructions A, B, C, D misread the construction, either via a new symmetry / cancellation theorem forbidding the offending counterterms or via a specific calculation showing that the divergent cocycles vanish in this theory.
- A local BPHZ-renormalizable bare action for the full nonlinear theory including the triple-graviton vertex, with explicit closure on a finite parameter set.
- A spectrum analysis of the relevant vacuum showing only healthy modes, addressing Barker's ghost finding.

I would welcome any of these. The writeup is meant as a structural map, not a verdict beyond appeal.

## 8. Conclusions

Partanen and Tulkki have written a serious paper that does not deliver what its headline claims, in a way that is structurally informative about the broader quantum-gravity terrain. The strict one-loop renormalizability claim is blocked by four independent obstructions: matter-loop Weyl-squared plus axial-current-quartic counterterms (Duff 1994 plus Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974); a paper-internal counterterm-basis mismatch documented at the level of equations 157–205; teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR one-parameter family (Casadio–Kuntz–Paci 2022); and a ghost-mode free-field spectrum specified by Barker (2023). The 2025 follow-ups expose a branch fork between gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG and TEGR-reduced presentations that the original paper blurred. Four coherent descendants exist; each abandons at least one stated goal.

The contribution here is not novel in its building blocks. Duff, Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen, Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux, Casadio–Kuntz–Paci, and Barker did the technical work. The contribution is their combination as applied to this specific construction, the paper-internal-equation backing of the counterterm-basis mismatch in obstruction B, the structural-impossibility framing tying the four obstructions to a single closure failure, the articulation of the branch fork as a central new finding in the 2025 follow-up record, and the four-descendant taxonomy with explicit trade-offs.

The program is alive as a research direction. It is not alive as the renormalizable quantum theory of gravity its headline announced. The most likely paths forward, if the authors want to preserve maximum continuity with their original construction, are descendant 5.1 (teleparallel EFT, abandoning strict renormalizability) or descendant 5.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct non-GR theory, abandoning the GR-equivalence framing). The author trajectory since 2025 is consistent with movement toward 5.4 without explicit framing. A clear branch choice and a fresh renormalization analysis on the chosen branch are the prerequisites for the program to make further serious progress.

I am posting this writeup with the goal of provoking either a refutation of one of the obstructions or a constructive engagement with the descendant taxonomy. Either outcome would be a contribution. The current state, popular-press celebration of a result that the technical record does not support, is not a good equilibrium for anyone, including the authors.
