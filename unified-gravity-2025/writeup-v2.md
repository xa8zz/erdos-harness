## TL;DR

Partanen and Tulkki's 2025 paper in *Reports on Progress in Physics* claims a one-loop renormalizable quantum theory of gravity unified with the Standard Model, organized as a $U(1)^4$ abelian gauge theory of a "space-time dimension field" with a teleparallel reduction. After reading the published version (arXiv:2310.01460v11 / Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 057802) and the five author follow-ups plus two corrigenda against the standard renormalization, BRST cohomology, and teleparallel literature, I do not think the strict headline claim is established on the present technical record. Four structural obstructions block it. The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 makes explicit a branch fork in the program's identity that the original paper blurred. Four coherent descendants remain, each abandoning at least one stated goal.

This is not a takedown. Partanen and Tulkki have written a serious paper, it survived peer review at a respected journal, and the program has generated specific falsifiable classical predictions (notably a 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection signature). What the published record does not yet support is a renormalizable quantum field theory of gravity in the BPHZ sense. The reading offered here is: the strict claim is not defensible on the present record, the program has coherent paths forward, and the public discussion needs an explicit branch choice.

A separate critique by Chishtie (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001) appears to attack the construction at a more primitive level than this writeup does, namely the Lorentz covariance of the $U(1)^4$ symmetry itself. I treat that critique as partially verifiable from publicly visible snippets and complementary to the obstructions below. A comparison table appears in §6.

## 1. The paper and what it claims

Partanen, M. and Tulkki, J., *Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model*, Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 057802 (2025); arXiv:2310.01460; DOI 10.1088/1361-6633/adc82e. The arXiv version went through eleven revisions between October 2023 and August 2025, with v11 about 270 times the size of v1. Most of the growth happened during 2025 peer review. Two corrigenda were published: Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 069501 (May 2025), correcting a factor of two in the projector tensor in equations 47, 109, 120, 142, and 162; and Rep. Prog. Phys. 88, 079501 (July 2025), correcting a single factor of $1/2$ in equation 122 with no downstream effect.

The construction takes an eight-spinor representation of the Lagrangian and introduces a "space-time dimension field." Its four $U(1)$ symmetries are gauged. The resulting theory contains four gauge potentials $H_{a\mu}$ with abelian field strengths $H_{a\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu H_{a\nu} - \partial_\nu H_{a\mu}$ (paper eqs. 39, 55–57). The gravity kinetic Lagrangian is a teleparallel-superpotential contraction,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa}\, H_{a\mu\nu} S^{a\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g},
$$
(eq. 61), where $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is the soldered superpotential built from $H$ and the tetrad. The paper warns explicitly that $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is not the field strength itself in a soldered gauge theory; this distinguishes the action from an abelian Pontryagin density. The graviton propagator in harmonic gauge is the standard linearized-GR result (eq. 138).

The three stated goals (paper section 9 and abstract):

1. **Perturbative renormalizability** at one loop, organized "in the same way as the gauge theories of the Standard Model."
2. **Classical reduction to GR** via the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (TEGR) in the Weitzenböck gauge, obtained from unified gravity by a gravity-gauge-field-dependent geometric condition.
3. **Equivalence principle** via equality of renormalized inertial and gravitational masses.

Popular-science coverage has been uniformly positive (zmescience, sciencealert, sciencedaily, Aalto press releases). Technical critique is sparser. Will Barker posted a spectrum analysis one month after v1 (arXiv:2311.11790, November 2023). Farrukh Chishtie published a critique in *Chinese Journal of Physics* in May 2026 framed as "nine interconnected structural limitations" (DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001). The full text is paywalled, but ScienceDirect snippets reveal a substantive partial structure of the argument; I return to this in §6. Bouali Bensouilah published a supportive tree-level calculation in *Canadian Journal of Physics* in 2026 (DOI 10.1139/cjp-2025-0362).

This writeup is a structural analysis of the renormalizability claim and its coherence. I do not dispute the classical existence of the construction. I argue that the strict version of goal (1) is not supported by the present technical record, and that the original presentation of goals (2) and (3) is in tension with the 2025 follow-ups.

## 2. Four obstructions to the strict one-loop renormalizability claim

Section 9 of the paper (renormalization) reads against the standard apparatus (BPHZ subtraction, local BRST cohomology in the sense of Barnich, Brandt, and Henneaux, and the teleparallel one-loop literature) as having four independent open issues. Each obstruction below builds on classical results; the contribution here is their combination as applied to this specific construction.

### Obstruction A: matter-loop Weyl-squared and axial-current-quartic counterterms

This is the textbook problem of matter-coupled gravity, repackaged in the paper's variables. A Dirac-fermion loop in a gravity background generates a Weyl-squared counterterm,
$$
\Delta \Gamma^{(1)}_{\text{div}} \supset \frac{b}{(4\pi)^2 \varepsilon} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
$$
with Duff coefficient $b = 1/20$ per Dirac fermion (Duff 1994). Under the paper's weak-field map $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{\kappa}\, h_{\mu\nu}[A]$, this pulls back to a four-derivative $(\partial F)^2$ operator of mass dimension 6 in the $U(1)^4$ gauge variables, not present in the bare $F^2$-type kinetic term. The Einstein–Dirac one-loop calculation additionally produces an irreducible axial-current quartic, $(\bar\psi \gamma^a \gamma_5 \psi)^4$, a dimension-12 eight-spinor operator (Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974, *Phys. Rev. D* **10**, 411). The axial current has dimension 3, so $(J_5)^4$ is genuinely dimension 12 before any factors of $\kappa$. The terminology "four-fermion" sometimes used for this operator is loose; it is more precisely an eight-spinor object.

Both counterterms are local, Lorentz invariant, gauge invariant, and BRST-closed at ghost number 0. By the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux classification (*Phys. Rep.* **338**, 439, 2000; arXiv:hep-th/0002245), they sit squarely in the admissible counterterm cohomology. BRST does not forbid them; it allows them. In an abelian theory with abelian gauge fixing, Slavnov–Taylor identities collapse to Ward identities, which constrain divergences to be gauge invariant but do not collapse independent gauge-invariant scalars into a single one. The bare action contains neither operator. No multiplicative $Z$-factor in the original parameter set can absorb a $p^4$ inverse propagator structure into a $p^2$ one, and no multiplicative $Z$ can absorb a dimension-12 operator into a dimension-4 mass term.

The statement here is not "therefore automatically fatal." It is: absent a new symmetry or cancellation theorem that forbids these admissible local counterterms, they are expected at one loop, and the paper has not supplied such a theorem. This obstruction is not specific to unified gravity. It applies to any two-derivative gravity theory coupled to fermionic matter in 4D, and has been known for fifty years. The paper does not address it; the renormalization analysis in section 9 restricts itself to the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational fields and leaves the broader Standard Model matter sector for future work. I return to the narrowness of the demonstrated result in §6.

### Obstruction B: paper-internal counterterm-basis mismatch

This is the decisive paper-internal critique. It does not require importing external results; the issue is visible in the paper's own one-loop formulas. Reading section 9.8 (graviton self-energy) against the bare counterterm structure in section 9.3–9.4, the mismatch is sharp.

The paper writes the bare field relation in eq. 157 as $H^{\mu\nu}_{\text{bare}} = \sqrt{Z_H}\, H^{\mu\nu}$, with the standard loop expansion $Z_H = 1 + \lambda \delta Z_H^{(1)} + \ldots$ (eq. 161). The renormalized gravity kinetic Lagrangian (eq. 162) is
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu} + (Z_H - 1) \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu},
$$
giving a counterterm vertex (eq. 189) of the form $i (Z_H - 1) p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}$. So the available bare quadratic counterterm has degree 2 in momentum and projector structure $\hat P_{1,2,1}$.

The one-loop graviton self-energy from the electron and photon loops (eqs. 187–188) has overall scaling $\kappa p^4 / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$:
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{electron}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{480 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log\frac{4\pi \mu^2 e^{-\gamma}}{m_e^2 c^2}\right] \hat P_B + \ldots,
$$
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{photon}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{240 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log(\ldots)\right] \hat P_{3,2,1} + \ldots
$$
where $\hat P_B = \hat P_{3,2,1} - 10 m_e^2 c^2 p^{-2} \hat P_{1,2,1} - 30 m_e^4 c^4 p^{-4} P_{1,0,1}$ (eq. 194). So the divergence sits in projectors $\hat P_{3,2,1}$ and $P_{1,0,1}$ as well as $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, and scales as $p^4$.

A constant $Z_H$ times a $p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}$ counterterm cannot absorb a $p^4 \hat P_{3,2,1}$ divergence. There is both a derivative-degree mismatch and a tensor-structure mismatch. The paper's resolution is in eqs. 203–205: impose the subtraction conditions
$$
\Xi_{1L} \Big|_{p^2 = 0} = 0, \qquad \left(\frac{\Xi_{1L}}{p^4}\right)\Big|_{p^2 \to \infty} = 0,
$$
and let $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ become explicitly momentum dependent, with functional form involving $p^2$, $m_e^2/p^2$, $m_e^4/p^4$, and logarithms. (A small notational caveat: the symbol $\Xi_{1L}$ in eq. 196 onward refers to a scalar propagator correction factor obtained by sandwiching the 1PI tensor between two tree propagators; this scalar object scales as $\kappa p^2$ and is distinct from the indexed 1PI tensor in eqs. 187–188. The displayed $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ formulas do not literally contain $\log p^2$; that logarithm appears in the renormalized remainder at eq. 207. Neither notational issue affects the substantive obstruction.)

In standard BPHZ and algebraic renormalization, the natural readings of this prescription are:

(i) Local enlargement of the counterterm basis with new higher-derivative gauge-invariant operators (schematically $H'_{a\mu\nu} \Box H'^{a\mu\nu}$), which is exactly what an EFT does. But then power-counting renormalizability has been abandoned.

(ii) Nonlocal subtraction at the propagator level, outside the BPHZ class. This is not what the standard "renormalizable like Yang–Mills" framing means.

The paper does neither. It uses momentum-dependent propagator-level subtraction while presenting it as multiplicative field renormalization. Read literally as a renormalization of the original local bare action, it is not Standard-Model-style BPHZ closure. This is the sharpest paper-internal issue and the one I would invite Partanen and Tulkki to address first.

**Referee-proofing: standard escapes that do not apply.** A sympathetic referee will ask whether the $p^4$ structure can be removed by routine means. Four standard escapes do not work here.

*Equations of motion.* On-shell removal would require the $p^4$ piece to vanish on the free graviton equations of motion. The divergence in eqs. 187–188 is computed off-shell, with the external graviton momentum $p$ unconstrained; the projectors are off-shell projectors in the BBH-Rivers sense. EOM redundancy cannot remove an off-shell counterterm needed to renormalize an off-shell 1PI function.

*Gauge artifact.* The projector decomposition into $\hat P_{3,2,1}$, $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, and $P_{1,0,1}$ is a decomposition into BRST-invariant Lorentz-covariant building blocks. Each projector is gauge-invariant in the sense relevant to local BRST cohomology. The mismatch is between two gauge-invariant pieces of the 1PI function, not between a gauge-fixed and a gauge-invariant piece.

*Projector degeneracy.* One might ask whether $\hat P_{3,2,1}$ and $\hat P_{1,2,1}$ could collapse to a single projector via on-shell or kinematic degeneracy. They cannot: the paper's own projector definitions (eqs. 190–195) construct them as independent transverse-traceless and trace projectors at off-shell momentum. They form an orthogonal basis on the relevant tensor space.

*Local field redefinition.* The most subtle escape. Ebert, Plefka, and Rodigast (arXiv:0710.1002, *Phys. Lett. B* 2007) showed that in Einstein–Yang–Mills, certain dimension-6 counterterms generated by mixed gravity–Yang–Mills loops can be removed by a local field redefinition of the gauge field, leading to the cancellation of gravitational contributions to the Yang–Mills beta function. That precedent is real, but it does not transfer to the case at hand. A single local field redefinition acts on one tensor structure; the divergence in eqs. 187–188 sits in multiple independent projectors ($\hat P_{3,2,1}$, $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, $P_{1,0,1}$) simultaneously, and the projector-decomposition coefficients are independent functions of $m_e^2/p^2$. No single local field redefinition of $H_{a\mu}$ can simultaneously cancel all three projector channels. The Ebert–Plefka–Rodigast mechanism removes one operator family; absorbing the unified-gravity divergence would require redefining away three independent local operators at once, which a single-field redefinition cannot do.

These checks do not rule out a more elaborate resolution, such as a new identity within the unified-gravity action that ties the three projector channels together. But the burden of demonstrating that identity sits on the authors, and the obvious escape routes are blocked.

### Obstruction C: teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR one-parameter family

The paper's goal (2), exact classical reduction to TEGR via a geometric condition $\eta = \eta(A)$, requires that the reduction hold off-shell at the quantum level, or it is not a stable identification. Casadio, Kuntz, and Paci (*Eur. Phys. J. C* **82**, 186, 2022; arXiv:2110.04325) settled what happens. Quantum matter loops on a teleparallel background generate one-loop divergences whose structure depends on the coupling prescription. Under full equivalence-principle coupling (translation plus local Lorentz), the divergences are quartic in torsion: the teleparallel image of $R^2$-type curvature-squared counterterms in metric variables. Under gauge-only translation coupling, the divergences are quadratic in torsion in the Yang–Mills contraction $T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu}$ alone, not in the specific linear combination
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{1}{4} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\nu\mu\rho} - T_\rho{}^{\rho\mu} T^\nu{}_{\nu\mu}
$$
that defines the TEGR torsion scalar (Aldrovandi–Pereira 2013; Maluf 2013, eqs. 23–27).

TEGR has one gravitational coupling, Newton's constant. A finite parameter redefinition cannot absorb an independent $T^2$ contraction or a $T^4$ term. Absorption requires enlargement to a three-parameter "New GR" action with independent coefficients $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ for the three torsion-quadratic invariants. That enlargement violates the off-shell closure of the $\eta = \eta(A)$ reduction.

Casadio–Kuntz–Paci do not work in Partanen–Tulkki's exact variables, so this is a relevance argument rather than a direct calculation in the unified-gravity formulation. The argument still goes through: even before quantizing the gravitational field itself, matter loops on a TEGR background fail to close into the TEGR action. The classical $\mathcal{T} = R$ (up to a boundary term) identity does not survive renormalization because it requires the specific 1:2:-1 ratio of coefficients, and that ratio is not radiatively stable.

### Obstruction D: Barker's spectrum analysis

Will Barker (Cambridge, now Czech Academy of Sciences) posted arXiv:2311.11790 one month after Partanen and Tulkki's v1. The abstract reports a "ghostly teleparallel vacuum" in the weak-field zero-coupling limit. The paper body, read carefully because the abstract summary is too compressed to act on, sharpens this into specific spin-mode structure.

In the symmetric interpretation of the unified-gravity weak-field zero-coupling limit, the two healthy graviton polarizations require one sign of $\kappa$ while five additional massless polarizations require the opposite sign of $\kappa$. Ghosts are unavoidable; no consistent choice of $\kappa$ makes all seven polarizations healthy simultaneously. In the asymmetric / teleparallel interpretation, Barker reports six ghost polarizations. In a bimetric reading of the same field content, the spectrum is still not unitary. He suggests bimetric, vacuum-expectation-value, or finite-coupling extensions as possible escape routes but does not establish that any of them works.

This is not a rigorous no-go theorem for every possible finite-coupling completion. It is a free-field-limit spectral analysis, and finite-coupling effects can in principle change the propagating mode content. It is a spectrum red flag, not a decisive proof of nonunitarity in every descendant. The honest statement is that the linearized theory does not obviously contain only healthy helicity-2 gravity plus gauge redundancy, and that the Partanen–Tulkki program needs an explicit unitary-spectrum demonstration in the physical branch. None has been published.

Barker's later work (arXiv:2406.12826 on Poincaré gauge theories; arXiv:2505.23894 on metric-affine gravity; arXiv:2506.02111 on parity-violating spectra; arXiv:2510.08201 on pure $R^2$ gravity) develops the same spectral-analysis machinery on related theories but does not re-engage Partanen and Tulkki by name. To my knowledge, Barker's 2023 paper is the only credentialed third-party arXiv critique of unified gravity in the spectrum-analysis line that has remained in the public discourse for two years without rebuttal.

## 3. Why the four obstructions cannot be addressed simultaneously inside the strict family

The obstructions are not four independent annoyances. They appear to be the same counterterm-basis-closure failure expressed in four different languages.

A says: matter loops generate spin-2 stress-tensor curvature-squared and axial-current-quartic counterterms not in the bare action.

B says: the paper's own one-loop self-energy has $p^4$ tensor structure not in the bare $p^2$ counterterm.

C says: TEGR's one-parameter family is not closed under matter renormalization.

D says: the free-theory spectrum at the relevant decoupling limit contains opposite-sign-$\kappa$ ghost modes.

A, B, and C reduce to the same statement: the bare action's parameter set is too small. D says: even before we worry about the bare action's parameters, the propagating modes are not all healthy.

I want to be careful about the standing of the next claim. What I will state is a structural inference, not a formal theorem. The argument has three inputs, each of which has solid published backing but none of which is itself an in-line calculation in Partanen–Tulkki's variables: (i) the explicit divergent-cocycle list in the standard 4D matter-coupled-gravity literature (Duff 1994, Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974, 't Hooft–Veltman 1974); (ii) the BBH cohomological classification of admissible counterterms; (iii) the paper-internal counterterm basis displayed in eqs. 157–205. From these, the natural reading is:

By the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux classification, every ghost-number-0 BRST cocycle that actually occurs in the one-loop divergence must already have an independent coupling in the bare action (stability of the action under renormalization). The divergent cocycles in 4D matter-coupled-gravity include $C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^2$, $R^2$, the torsion-quadratic invariants beyond $\mathcal{T}$, the torsion-quartic invariants, and the axial-current quartic. The original bare action contains none of them. Adding them enlarges the family. Refusing to add them leaves the divergences uncanceled.

The structural reading is then: no local-BPHZ-renormalizable descendant within the strict original family can absorb A, B, C, D simultaneously while preserving all three stated goals at original strength. At least one of (exact TEGR closure, two-derivative gravitational dynamics, finite-coupling renormalizability, healthy free spectrum, $U(1)^4$ field-space organization) must be abandoned.

This is a structural inference from published results, not a closed proof. A counterexample would require either showing that some of the "divergent" cocycles actually vanish in this construction (which would need a new symmetry or cancellation theorem not present in the published record), or showing that one of the four obstructions misreads the construction. I have looked for both and not found them. Three rounds of independent GPT Pro dispatches in the research program behind this writeup agreed on the structural reading via different framings and emphases. That is within-family convergence, not cross-family verification, and I treat it accordingly: as suggestive but not conclusive. I would welcome an attempt at refutation.

## 4. The branch fork: what the 2025 follow-ups make explicit

The most consequential new finding in this writeup did not come from re-reading the original paper. It came from reading the July 2025 follow-up, arXiv:2507.07790, "Extending unified gravity to account for graviton-graviton interaction." The follow-up exposes a structural fork in the program's identity that the original paper papered over.

Three direct quotes from arXiv:2507.07790 (verified against the html v2 of the preprint):

> There is no equivalence transformation from unified gravity to TEGR.

> The substitutions that produce TEGR break the gauge symmetry of unified gravity.

> The new gauge-invariant graviton-graviton self-interaction term does not contribute to the TEGR-reduced Lagrangian.

And on renormalization, the same paper closes with:

> Regarding the Feynman diagrams of UG, our extension introduces the triple-graviton vertex, whose implications to the renormalization of UG are left as a topic of further work.

These four sentences are load-bearing. They mean the program is not one theory with two equivalent presentations. It is two structurally distinct theories connected by a symmetry-breaking substitution:

**The Minkowski-UG branch** preserves the four $U(1)$ gauge symmetry, contains the new triple-graviton vertex introduced in 2507.07790, and carries the 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection deviations predicted in arXiv:2505.14446. Its renormalization properties are unknown; the authors explicitly defer them. Its tree-level phenomenology is genuinely non-GR.

**The TEGR-reduced branch** is recovered from the Minkowski-UG branch by substitutions that break the gauge symmetry. It does not contain the new graviton-graviton self-interaction term in the same form (the extension term vanishes under the TEGR reduction, per the third quote above). It classically agrees with GR.

The original paper's framing ("we have a theory that reproduces GR via the teleparallel equivalence and also predicts new effects") is in tension as a unified statement once these two branches are distinguished. The 23.3% / 26.7% deviations belong to the Minkowski-UG branch specifically. They are not predictions of a theory that classically reduces to GR. They are predictions of a separate theory connected to GR only through a substitution that breaks its own gauge symmetry.

The branch fork also reframes the renormalization question. The original section 9 analysis was carried out in the UGM/Minkowski formulation, on a theory without graviton self-interaction. The 2025 follow-up admits that graviton self-interaction is a necessary extension of the Minkowski theory; quoting the same paper, "the new gauge-invariant graviton-graviton self-interaction term" is required for problems involving interactions between gravitational fields. So the theory whose one-loop renormalization was analyzed in 2310.01460 is not the theory the authors now regard as physically necessary. The renormalization analysis must be restarted on the extended theory. The 2025 follow-up acknowledges this and defers it.

That is a substantial reframing. The original headline claim, even setting aside obstructions A through D, was renormalization of a sector of an incomplete theory: the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational fields in the Minkowski formulation without graviton self-interaction. The paper's section 9 itself flags the matter-sector restriction; it does not claim renormalization of the full Standard Model coupled to gravity. The popular-press framing, that unified gravity solves the problem of quantum gravity unified with the Standard Model, overstates what the paper demonstrates.

## 5. Four coherent descendants

The strict claim does not survive on the present record, but the program is not empty. Four structurally distinct descendants remain coherent, each abandoning at least one original goal.

### 5.1 Teleparallel / tetrad effective field theory

The most conservative descendant. Take the bare action to contain the full local operator basis allowed by symmetries: torsion-quadratic terms with independent coefficients ($c_1, c_2, c_3$), torsion-quartic terms, curvature-squared terms ($\alpha_C C^2 + \alpha_R R^2 + \alpha_G G$), and the higher-dimensional fermionic operator tower including the axial-current quartic $(J_5^\mu J_{5\mu})^2$. Match the leading torsion-quadratic coefficients to the TEGR values $(c_1, c_2, c_3) = (1/4, 1/2, -1)$ at low energies. Treat higher-derivative terms as perturbative insertions, not resummed propagator poles.

This is Donoghue–Burgess EFT of gravity (Donoghue 1994, *Phys. Rev. D* **50**, 3874; Burgess 2004 *Living Reviews* **7**, 5) with a teleparallel parameterization. It preserves the classical GR/TEGR limit and a low-energy form of the equivalence principle (universal coupling to the stress-energy tensor below the cutoff). It abandons strict perturbative renormalizability in the Yang–Mills sense; the theory is predictive order by order in $E/\Lambda_*$ rather than to all orders with a finite parameter set.

This is the closest descendant to the original spirit of the construction.

A separate EFT-framed unification program exists in the public record. Chishtie's USMEG-EFT framework (arXiv:2503.09796, *Canadian J. Phys.* accepted) reaches an EFT-based unification conclusion via different machinery, namely Lagrange-multiplier-restricted GR plus Appelquist–Carazzone decoupling rather than teleparallel counterterm enlargement. USMEG-EFT is a competing EFT-style exit route with its own strong claims ("first successful unification," "fully renormalizable and EFT framework"). It should not be read as corroboration of the equation-level obstructions below, since it makes independent technical claims of its own. The honest framing is that two structurally similar exit routes (EFT-style enlargement to absorb the matter-coupled-gravity divergences) have been reached by independent authors via different machinery. Their conclusions are structurally aligned; their derivations are not the same.

### 5.2 New-GR teleparallel renormalization

A narrower descendant that addresses obstruction C alone. Promote the three torsion-quadratic coefficients to independent renormalized parameters but stop short of including the full higher-derivative tower. This is Casadio–Kuntz–Paci's "easy fix" at one loop. It abandons exact TEGR closure (the 1:2:-1 ratio is no longer protected) but keeps teleparallel structure.

The descendant has known structural risks. Beltrán Jiménez, Heisenberg, and Koivisto (*Phys. Lett. B* **805**, 135422, 2020) showed that generic teleparallel quadratic theories around Minkowski typically contain extra symmetric rank-2 fields plus a 2-form, and that the linear symmetries needed for viability can fail nonlinearly, producing strong-coupling or pathological behavior. New GR is a good leading EFT basis. It is not an established UV completion. A separate spectrum and stability check is required before this descendant can claim more than the EFT descendant in §5.1.

### 5.3 Higher-derivative curvature gravity with fakeons

The descendant that preserves perturbative renormalizability most strongly. Take the bare action in Stelle / Fradkin–Tseytlin form,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{QG}} = \sqrt{-g}\left[-\Lambda - \frac{\bar M_P^2}{2} R + \alpha (R_{\mu\nu} R^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{3} R^2) - \frac{\xi}{6} R^2 \right] + \mathcal{L}_{\text{matter}}.
$$
Stelle (1977, *Phys. Rev. D* **16**, 953) proved renormalizability. Fradkin and Tseytlin (1982, *Nucl. Phys. B* **201**, 469) proved asymptotic freedom of the essential couplings. The $1/p^4$ UV behavior of the graviton propagator makes the theory power-counting renormalizable with general renormalizable matter sectors.

The standard problem is a massive spin-2 ghost in the propagator. Anselmi and Piva (2018, arXiv:1803.07777) showed that the fakeon prescription quantizes the massive spin-2 pole as a fakeon rather than a physical ghost, preserving perturbative unitarity at the cost of modified microcausality above the fakeon scale. The fakeon prescription does not affect renormalization (it acts at the propagator-pole level only); the beta functions and one-loop counterterms are computed explicitly.

This descendant abandons exact TEGR closure entirely; GR survives only as an IR / classical limit, not as the UV theory. It abandons the "equal renormalized inertial and gravitational masses" mechanism in favor of standard diffeomorphism-invariant universal coupling. It abandons standard microcausality above the fakeon scale. What it gains is the strongest available version of goal (1): gravity on the same perturbative footing as renormalizable matter sectors.

### 5.4 Minkowski-UG as a distinct gauge theory

The descendant the 2025 follow-ups appear to be moving toward, without yet stating it explicitly. Drop the rhetoric of GR equivalence. Treat the gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch as a distinct classical and quantum theory of gravity on its own merits. The right benchmarks are then not "does it reproduce GR" but the standard local-QFT health checks applied to this specific theory:

- Healthy particle spectrum around the relevant vacuum (addressing Barker).
- Local counterterm basis closure for the full theory including the triple-graviton vertex (addressing B and the 2025 deferral).
- Viable Ward / Slavnov–Taylor identities.
- Consistent EFT expansion.
- Experimentally viable 2PN polarization predictions (addressing 2505.14446's 23.3% / 26.7% signature).

Bensouilah's 2026 calculation of tree-level photon-photon scattering in UGM, reproducing the classical Tolman–Ehrenfest–Podolsky description of the gravitational field produced by light, is a useful consistency check inside this descendant. The atomic-redshift, perihelion-precession, and light-deflection follow-ups by Partanen, Tulkki, and collaborators (arXiv:2505.14446, 2506.14447, 2506.22057) are also natural inhabitants of this descendant.

This is an open research program, not a completed quantum gravity solution. The 2PN polarization signature is genuinely non-GR. In classical metric GR plus Maxwell theory, ray-optics light propagation is polarization-blind; the known polarization-dependent gravitational effects (spin-optics, helicity corrections, and Drummond–Hathrell QED vacuum polarization in curved space) are all sub-leading to ray optics, not 23% effects at second post-Newtonian order. It is testable via LATOR-class missions. Whether the underlying theory survives the local-QFT health checks is open.

**Independent technical critique.** Two credentialed third-party critiques exist in the public record.

*Barker* (arXiv:2311.11790, November 2023) is the ghost-spectrum analysis covered in obstruction D. It is about whether the propagating modes in the weak-field zero-coupling limit are all healthy. It is not about whether the gauge construction gives the correct spin representation at all.

*Chishtie* (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001) is framed as "nine interconnected structural limitations." The full text is not publicly accessible to me: ScienceDirect blocks full access and ResearchGate lists no full-text PDF. However, ScienceDirect snippets reveal several of the nine claimed limitations: Step 1 "Incompatible transformation requirements," Step 2 "Component-wise phase rotation is Lorentz non-covariant," an "inescapable dilemma" between Steps 1 and 2, a claim that the UGM covariant derivative carries a Lorentz-non-covariant residual, Step 6 a spin-content objection (claiming UGM describes spin-1 rather than spin-2 gravitational particles), and Step 7 a Ward-identities-and-non-renormalizability argument. I treat Chishtie as a partially verifiable, independently convergent critique, while not relying on its unpublished details.

Chishtie's visible argument attacks UGM structurally upstream of the obstructions here. The visible Lorentz-covariance objection (Steps 1, 2, and the covariant-derivative residual) is about whether $U(1)^4$ acting component-wise on objects carrying spacetime/Lorentz index structure is even a Lorentz-covariant internal gauge symmetry. If it is not, then the Ward and BRST identities the renormalization argument relies on would not be identities of a Lorentz-covariant gravitational gauge theory, and the entire counterterm-basis discussion below sits on shakier ground. I do not rely on this objection because I cannot read the full proof, but if it is correct, it precedes the four obstructions.

Chishtie's Step 6 spin-content objection is distinct from Barker's spectrum analysis. Barker is about ghost vs. healthy modes in the weak-field zero-coupling limit (do the polarizations have the right sign for $\kappa$?). Chishtie's Step 6 is about whether the gauge construction yields the correct spin representation at all (does $U(1)^4$ give spin-2 or spin-1?). They are orthogonal objections. They should not be collapsed.

## 7. What is supported, what is open, what would change my mind

**Strongly supported (my current conclusion, after the analysis above):**

- The strict one-loop renormalizability claim in the original family is not defensible on the present technical record. Obstructions A, B, C, D appear to be independent failures of the same closure condition.
- The original section 9 analysis covers only the electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational sector, not the full Standard Model coupled to gravity.
- The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 makes explicit a branch fork between the gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch and the TEGR-reduced branch. They are not equivalent presentations of one theory.
- The 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection signature is a prediction of the Minkowski-UG branch specifically, not of "unified gravity" as a unified concept.
- Four coherent descendants exist (§§5.1–5.4), each abandoning at least one stated goal.

**Open:**

- The full content of Chishtie's "nine interconnected structural limitations" (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026). I cannot evaluate the remaining four to five steps without access to the paper.
- Cross-family verification (Gemini DeepThink, Claude with extended thinking) of the within-Pro convergence behind this writeup. The dispatches behind this writeup were within a single model family; this is suggestive but not conclusive.
- The full loop renormalization analysis of the extended Minkowski-UG theory with the triple-graviton vertex from 2507.07790. The authors defer it.
- The future-experimental status of the 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection prediction. LATOR-class missions could test it.
- Whether descendant 5.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct theory) survives the standard local-QFT health checks. This is the open research question.

**What would change my mind on the strict claim:**

- A demonstration that obstructions A, B, C, D misread the construction, either via a new symmetry or cancellation theorem forbidding the offending counterterms or via a specific calculation showing that the divergent cocycles vanish in this theory.
- A local BPHZ-renormalizable bare action for the full nonlinear theory including the triple-graviton vertex, with explicit closure on a finite parameter set.
- A spectrum analysis of the relevant vacuum showing only healthy modes, addressing Barker's ghost finding.
- A direct rebuttal of the four standard escapes in Obstruction B (off-shell EOM, gauge artifact, projector degeneracy, single-field redefinition) that identifies a fifth route I have not anticipated.

I would welcome any of these. The writeup is meant as a structural map, not a verdict beyond appeal.

## 8. Conclusions

Partanen and Tulkki have written a serious paper that does not, on the present technical record, deliver what its headline claims, in a way that is structurally informative about the broader quantum-gravity terrain. The strict one-loop renormalizability claim is blocked, as far as I can determine, by four independent obstructions: matter-loop Weyl-squared plus axial-current-quartic counterterms (Duff 1994 plus Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974); a paper-internal counterterm-basis mismatch documented at the level of equations 157–205; teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR one-parameter family (Casadio–Kuntz–Paci 2022); and a ghost-mode free-field spectrum specified by Barker (2023). The 2025 follow-ups make explicit a branch fork between gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG and TEGR-reduced presentations that the original paper blurred. Four coherent descendants exist; each abandons at least one stated goal.

The contribution here is not novel in its building blocks. Duff, Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen, Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux, Casadio–Kuntz–Paci, and Barker did the technical work. The contribution, conditional on Chishtie 2026's full text not pre-empting it, is the combination of those building blocks as applied to this specific construction, the paper-internal-equation backing of the counterterm-basis mismatch in obstruction B with referee-proofing against the standard escapes, the structural inference tying the four obstructions to a single closure failure, the articulation of the branch fork as a central new finding in the 2025 follow-up record, and the four-descendant taxonomy with explicit trade-offs.

The program is alive as a research direction. The published record does not yet support its identification as the renormalizable quantum theory of gravity the headline announced. The most likely paths forward, if the authors want to preserve maximum continuity with their original construction, are descendant 5.1 (teleparallel EFT, abandoning strict renormalizability) or descendant 5.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct non-GR theory, abandoning the GR-equivalence framing). The author trajectory since 2025 is consistent with movement toward 5.4 without explicit framing. A clear branch choice and a fresh renormalization analysis on the chosen branch are the prerequisites for further serious progress.

I am posting this writeup with the goal of provoking either a refutation of one of the obstructions or a constructive engagement with the descendant taxonomy. Either outcome would be a contribution. The gap between the popular framing and the technical status is wide enough that an explicit accounting on the record is overdue.
