## TL;DR

Partanen and Tulkki's 2025 paper in *Reports on Progress in Physics* presents unified gravity as a one-loop renormalizable quantum theory of gravity, organized as a $U(1)^4$ abelian gauge theory of a "space-time dimension field" with a teleparallel reduction, and frames it as "in the same way as the gauge theories of the Standard Model." Two facts about the published record are easy to verify directly and frame everything that follows.

*First, the paper's own §9.2 restricts its one-loop renormalization analysis to the Dirac electron-positron field, the electromagnetic field, and the gravitational field.* The full Standard Model matter sector (quarks, weak bosons, Higgs, color) is not included. The headline result is a one-loop analysis of a small subsystem, not a Standard-Model-plus-gravity renormalization proof.

*Second, the paper's published renormalization prescription is not standard BPHZ closure of the original local action.* The bare counterterm structure (eq. 162) is a constant $Z_H$ multiplying a $p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}$ projector; the one-loop graviton self-energy (eqs. 187–188) diverges as $\kappa p^4 / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$ in independent projectors $\hat P_{3,2,1}$, $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, $P_{1,0,1}$. The paper's resolution (eqs. 203–205) lets $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ become a nontrivial function of $p^2$ and $m_e^2/p^2$. Read literally as a renormalization of the original local bare action, this is either local enlargement of the operator basis (then strict renormalizability has been abandoned) or nonlocal propagator-level subtraction (then BPHZ does not apply). The paper's framing as multiplicative field renormalization in the Standard-Model sense is, on my reading, not what is actually being done.

I argue this is the central obstruction, and I give it as a self-contained equation-level critique in §2. Three further obstructions — matter-loop Weyl-squared and axial-current-quartic counterterms (§3), teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR one-parameter family (§4), and a ghost-mode free-field spectrum (§5) — are imported from adjacent literatures with different evidentiary statuses. The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 makes explicit a structural fork the original paper did not separate, between a gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch and a TEGR-reduced branch obtained by symmetry-breaking substitutions (§7). Four coherent descendants of the program remain, each abandoning at least one originally stated goal (§8).

The purpose is diagnostic, not polemical. The construction is well posed at the classical level and the program has produced specific falsifiable predictions, notably a 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection signature within the authors' Minkowski-UG formulation. What the published record does not establish, on my reading, is one-loop renormalizability in the BPHZ sense for the full Standard Model coupled to gravity.

## 1. The target paper and conventions

Partanen, M. and Tulkki, J., *Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model*, Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 057802 (2025); arXiv:2310.01460; DOI 10.1088/1361-6633/adc82e. Two corrigenda were published: Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 069501 (May 2025), correcting a factor of two in the projector tensor across equations 47, 109, 120, 142, 162; and Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 079501 (July 2025), correcting a single factor of $1/2$ in equation 122 with no downstream effect.

**Notation.** I use UG for the construction in general; UGM for the Minkowski-metric formulation specifically; *Minkowski-UG branch* for the gauge-invariant theory preserved by the 2025 follow-up extension; and *TEGR-reduced branch* for the theory recovered by the symmetry-breaking substitutions discussed in §7. I write $U(1)^4$ for the gauge group throughout (equivalent to $4 \times U(1)$, used interchangeably by the original authors). BPHZ denotes Bogoliubov–Parasiuk–Hepp–Zimmermann subtraction. BRST denotes Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin cohomology. TEGR denotes the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity. 1PI denotes one-particle irreducible. 2PN denotes second post-Newtonian.

**Equation references.** All equation numbers refer to the published version Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 057802 (2025), as corrected by the two corrigenda Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 069501 and **88**, 079501. The same numbering appears in arXiv:2310.01460v11.

**Construction.** The theory takes an eight-spinor representation of the Lagrangian and introduces a "space-time dimension field" whose four $U(1)$ symmetries are gauged. The resulting theory contains four gauge potentials $H_{a\mu}$ with abelian field strengths $H_{a\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu H_{a\nu} - \partial_\nu H_{a\mu}$ (paper eqs. 39, 55–57). The gravity kinetic Lagrangian is the teleparallel-superpotential contraction
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa}\, H_{a\mu\nu} S^{a\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g},
$$
(eq. 61), where $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is the soldered superpotential built from $H$ and the tetrad. The paper warns explicitly that $S^{a\mu\nu}$ is not the field strength itself in a soldered gauge theory; the action is not an abelian Pontryagin density and is dynamical. The graviton propagator in harmonic gauge (eq. 138) is the standard linearized-GR result.

**Stated goals (paper §9 and abstract).**

1. Perturbative renormalizability at one loop, "in the same way as the gauge theories of the Standard Model."
2. Classical reduction to GR via TEGR in the Weitzenböck gauge, obtained from unified gravity by a gravity-gauge-field-dependent geometric condition.
3. Equivalence principle via equality of renormalized inertial and gravitational masses.

The narrowness of goal (1) as actually demonstrated is the entry point: §9.2 of the paper restricts the one-loop analysis to electron-positron, electromagnetic, and gravitational fields. The remaining four obstructions and the branch fork follow.

## 2. Direct obstruction: counterterm-basis mismatch (Obstruction B)

This is the strongest paper-internal critique and the one I am most confident in. It does not require importing external results; the mismatch is visible in the paper's own one-loop formulas.

### 2.0 Criterion for local one-loop counterterm closure

Before turning to the mismatch itself, it helps to state explicitly the renormalization criterion against which the displayed Partanen–Tulkki prescription will be measured.

In local renormalization, whether one works in BPHZ subtraction or its algebraic-renormalization reformulation, counterterms must be local polynomials in momenta and derivatives, built from operators compatible with the symmetries of the theory. Renormalization constants depend on the renormalization scale $\mu$, on couplings, masses, and gauge parameters; they do not depend on the external momentum $p$ as a continuous variable. A momentum-subtraction (MOM) scheme still produces constants defined at a chosen renormalization point. It does not treat $Z$ as a continuous function $Z(p^2)$ unless one is implicitly enlarging the operator basis or resumming an infinite tower of higher-derivative operators. A constant $Z$ may multiply a fixed local operator at fixed derivative order, but it cannot interpolate between derivative orders or between independent projector channels.

This is the criterion. §2 of the present comment then asks whether the displayed Partanen–Tulkki prescription, read as a renormalization of the original local bare action, meets it.

### 2.1 The mismatch

The paper writes the bare field relation (eq. 157) as $H^{\mu\nu}_{\text{bare}} = \sqrt{Z_H}\, H^{\mu\nu}$, with the standard loop expansion $Z_H = 1 + \lambda \delta Z_H^{(1)} + \ldots$ (eq. 161). The renormalized gravity kinetic Lagrangian (eq. 162) is
$$
\mathcal{L}_{g,\text{kin}} = \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu} + (Z_H - 1) \frac{1}{4\kappa} H_{\rho\mu\nu} S^{\rho\mu\nu},
$$
producing a counterterm vertex (eq. 189) of the form $i (Z_H - 1) p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}$. The available bare quadratic counterterm has degree 2 in momentum and projector structure $\hat P_{1,2,1}$.

The one-loop graviton self-energy from electron and photon loops (eqs. 187–188) scales as $\kappa p^4 / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$:
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{electron}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{480 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log\frac{4\pi \mu^2 e^{-\gamma}}{m_e^2 c^2}\right] \hat P_B + \ldots,
$$
$$
i \Xi^{\alpha\beta,\eta\lambda}_{1L,\text{photon}} = \frac{i \kappa c\, p^4}{240 \pi^2 \hbar}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\text{UV}}} + \log(\ldots)\right] \hat P_{3,2,1} + \ldots
$$
where $\hat P_B = \hat P_{3,2,1} - 10 m_e^2 c^2 p^{-2} \hat P_{1,2,1} - 30 m_e^4 c^4 p^{-4} P_{1,0,1}$ (eq. 194). The divergence sits in projectors $\hat P_{3,2,1}$, $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, and $P_{1,0,1}$, and scales as $p^4$.

A momentum-independent $Z_H$ multiplying a $p^2 \hat P_{1,2,1}$ counterterm cannot absorb a $p^4 \hat P_{3,2,1}$ divergence. There is both a derivative-degree mismatch and a tensor-structure mismatch. The mismatch is summarized in the following table.

**Table 1. Bare counterterm vs. one-loop divergence structure.**

| | Bare quadratic counterterm (eq. 189) | One-loop self-energy divergence (eqs. 187–188) |
|---|---|---|
| Overall scale | $(Z_H - 1)$, constant in $p$ | $\kappa / \varepsilon_{\text{UV}}$, plus $\log$ |
| Derivative order in $p$ | $p^2$ | $p^4$ |
| Projector content | $\hat P_{1,2,1}$ only | $\hat P_{3,2,1}$, $\hat P_{1,2,1}$, $P_{1,0,1}$, with coefficients functions of $m_e^2/p^2$ |
| Locality of counterterm | local (multiplicative) | requires functions of $m_e^2/p^2, m_e^4/p^4$ to subtract |
| Why a constant $Z_H$ cannot absorb the divergence | A constant scalar $\times$ a fixed projector at fixed derivative order cannot match a higher-derivative-order $p^4$ structure in independent projector channels. | The three projectors are linearly independent on the relevant tensor space; the coefficients are independent functions of $m_e^2/p^2$. |

The paper's resolution is in eqs. 203–205: impose the subtraction conditions
$$
\Xi_{1L} \Big|_{p^2 = 0} = 0, \qquad \left(\frac{\Xi_{1L}}{p^4}\right)\Big|_{p^2 \to \infty} = 0,
$$
and let $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ become explicitly momentum dependent, with functional form involving $p^2$, $m_e^2/p^2$, $m_e^4/p^4$, and logarithms. (A small notational caveat: the symbol $\Xi_{1L}$ in eq. 196 onward refers to a scalar propagator-correction factor obtained by sandwiching the 1PI tensor between two tree propagators, distinct from the indexed 1PI tensor in eqs. 187–188. The displayed $\delta Z_H^{(1)}$ formulas do not literally contain $\log p^2$; that logarithm appears in the renormalized remainder at eq. 207. Neither notational issue affects the substantive obstruction.)

By the criterion stated in §2.0, this prescription reads naturally as one of two things, neither of which is what the paper claims:

(i) *Local enlargement of the counterterm basis* with new higher-derivative gauge-invariant operators (schematically $H'_{a\mu\nu} \Box H'^{a\mu\nu}$). This is exactly what an effective field theory does. But then power-counting renormalizability has been abandoned; the theory becomes Donoghue–Burgess EFT in different variables.

(ii) *Nonlocal subtraction at the propagator level*, outside the BPHZ class.

The paper does not explicitly identify the prescription as either. Read literally as a renormalization of the original local bare action, it is not Standard-Model-style BPHZ closure. This is the strongest paper-internal critique and the one I invite Partanen and Tulkki to address first.

### Possible objections and why they do not resolve the mismatch

A sympathetic referee will ask whether the $p^4$ structure can be removed by standard equivalences. Four routes do not work here.

**Equations of motion.** On-shell removal would require the $p^4$ piece to vanish on the free graviton equations of motion. The divergence in eqs. 187–188 is computed off-shell, with the external graviton momentum $p$ unconstrained; the projectors are off-shell projectors in the BBH–Rivers sense. Equation-of-motion redundancy cannot remove an off-shell counterterm needed to renormalize an off-shell 1PI function.

**Gauge artifact.** The projector decomposition isolates independent Lorentz-covariant tensor channels in the off-shell two-point function. If these divergent channels are not BRST-exact or removable by field redefinition, they require corresponding local counterterms. The mismatch is between independent gauge-invariant pieces of the 1PI function, not between a gauge-fixed and a gauge-invariant piece.

**Projector degeneracy.** The paper's own projector definitions (eqs. 190–195) construct $\hat P_{3,2,1}$ and $\hat P_{1,2,1}$ as independent transverse-traceless and trace projectors at off-shell momentum. They form an orthogonal basis on the relevant tensor space and do not collapse to a single projector via on-shell or kinematic degeneracy.

**Local field redefinition.** The most subtle escape. Ebert, Plefka, and Rodigast (*Phys. Lett. B* **660**, 579, 2008; arXiv:0710.1002) showed that in Einstein–Yang–Mills, certain dimension-6 counterterms from mixed gravity–Yang–Mills loops can be removed by a local field redefinition of the gauge field, eliminating gravitational contributions to the Yang–Mills beta function. The precedent is real but does not transfer. A single local field redefinition acts on one tensor structure; the divergence in eqs. 187–188 sits in three independent projectors simultaneously, with projector-decomposition coefficients that are independent functions of $m_e^2/p^2$. No single local field redefinition of $H_{a\mu}$ can simultaneously cancel all three projector channels.

These checks do not rule out a more elaborate resolution, such as a new identity within the unified-gravity action tying the three projector channels together. A resolution would require an additional identity specific to unified gravity that relates these projector channels; no such identity is identified in the published analysis. These standard escape routes do not, on their face, resolve the mismatch.

## 3. Supporting expectation from matter-coupled gravity (Obstruction A)

This is the standard counterterm problem of matter-coupled gravity, expressed in the paper's variables. It is relevant because it concerns operators the paper has not analyzed, in matter sectors the paper has deferred.

A Dirac-fermion loop in a gravity background generates a Weyl-squared counterterm,
$$
\Delta \Gamma^{(1)}_{\text{div}} \supset \frac{b}{(4\pi)^2 \varepsilon} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
$$
with Duff coefficient $b = 1/20$ per Dirac fermion (Duff 1994). Under the paper's weak-field map $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{\kappa}\, h_{\mu\nu}[A]$, this pulls back to a four-derivative $(\partial F)^2$ operator of mass dimension 6 in the $U(1)^4$ gauge variables, not present in the bare $F^2$-type kinetic term. The Einstein–Dirac one-loop calculation additionally produces an irreducible axial-current quartic, $(\bar\psi \gamma^a \gamma_5 \psi)^4$, a dimension-12 eight-spinor operator (Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974, *Phys. Rev. D* **10**, 411). The axial current has dimension 3, so $(J_5)^4$ is dimension 12 before any factors of $\kappa$.

Verbatim, the Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974 abstract describes "diagrams containing eight external fermions yielding a divergence proportional to the fourth power of the fermion axial-vector current," which is $(J_5)^4$ in our notation — a dimension-12 eight-spinor operator. This is distinct from the dimension-6 four-fermion contact term $(J_5^\mu J_{5\mu})$ that appears in some later treatments and is sometimes loosely called the "four-fermion" Einstein–Dirac counterterm. The original Einstein–Dirac one-loop divergence is the eight-spinor operator, which is the object that matters for the counterterm-basis question here.

Both counterterms are local, Lorentz invariant, gauge invariant, and BRST closed at ghost number 0. By the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux classification (*Phys. Rep.* **338**, 439, 2000), they sit squarely in the admissible counterterm cohomology. BRST does not forbid them; it allows them. In an abelian theory with abelian gauge fixing, Slavnov–Taylor identities collapse to Ward identities, which constrain divergences to be gauge invariant but do not collapse independent gauge-invariant scalars into a single one. The bare action contains neither operator. A multiplicative $Z$-factor in the original parameter set cannot absorb a $p^4$ inverse-propagator structure into a $p^2$ one, and cannot absorb a dimension-12 operator into a dimension-4 mass term.

This observation is not by itself a no-go theorem. The statement is: absent a new symmetry or cancellation theorem forbidding these admissible local counterterms, they are expected at one loop, and the paper has not supplied such a theorem. The obstruction is not specific to unified gravity; it applies to any two-derivative gravity theory coupled to fermionic matter in 4D, and has been known for fifty years. The paper acknowledges the matter-sector restriction in §9.2 and leaves the broader Standard Model for future work. A fuller treatment would have to address Weyl-squared from photons (Duff coefficient $1/10$), color, weak bosons, Higgs, and quarks — none of which are in the paper's one-loop analysis.

## 4. Supporting analogy from teleparallel one-loop renormalization (Obstruction C)

Goal (2), exact classical reduction to TEGR via a geometric condition $\eta = \eta(A)$, requires the reduction to hold off-shell at the quantum level, or it is not a stable identification. Casadio, Kuntz, and Paci (*Eur. Phys. J. C* **82**, 186, 2022; arXiv:2110.04325) analyzed the relevant one-loop problem in teleparallel backgrounds. Under full equivalence-principle coupling (translation plus local Lorentz), the divergences are quartic in torsion: the teleparallel image of $R^2$-type curvature-squared counterterms in metric variables. Under gauge-only translation coupling, the divergences are quadratic in torsion in the Yang–Mills contraction $T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu}$ alone, not in the specific linear combination
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{1}{4} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\rho\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2} T^{\rho\mu\nu} T_{\nu\mu\rho} - T_\rho{}^{\rho\mu} T^\nu{}_{\nu\mu}
$$
that defines the TEGR torsion scalar (Aldrovandi–Pereira 2013; Maluf 2013, eqs. 23–27).

TEGR has one gravitational coupling, Newton's constant. A finite parameter redefinition cannot absorb an independent $T^2$ contraction or a $T^4$ term. Absorption requires enlargement to a three-parameter "New GR" action with independent coefficients $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ for the three torsion-quadratic invariants. That enlargement violates the off-shell closure of the $\eta = \eta(A)$ reduction.

Casadio–Kuntz–Paci do not work in Partanen–Tulkki's exact variables, so this is a relevance argument rather than a direct calculation. The result is highly relevant, though not identical to a calculation in unified-gravity variables. Even before quantizing the gravitational field, matter loops on a TEGR background fail to close into the TEGR action. The classical identity $\mathcal{T} = R$ (up to a boundary term) does not survive renormalization because it requires the specific $(1/4, 1/2, -1)$ ratio of coefficients, and that ratio is not radiatively stable.

## 5. Independent spectral warning (Obstruction D)

Will Barker posted arXiv:2311.11790 in November 2023, one month after the first arXiv version of Partanen–Tulkki. The abstract reports a "ghostly teleparallel vacuum" in the weak-field zero-coupling limit. The paper body sharpens this into specific spin-mode structure.

In the symmetric interpretation of the unified-gravity weak-field zero-coupling limit, the two healthy graviton polarizations require one sign of $\kappa$ while five additional massless polarizations require the opposite sign of $\kappa$. Ghosts are unavoidable; no consistent choice of $\kappa$ makes all seven polarizations healthy simultaneously. In the asymmetric / teleparallel interpretation, Barker reports six ghost polarizations. In a bimetric reading, the spectrum is still not unitary. He suggests bimetric, vacuum-expectation-value, or finite-coupling extensions as possible escape routes but does not establish that any of them works.

This should be treated as a spectral warning rather than a complete no-go theorem. It is a free-field-limit spectral analysis, and finite-coupling effects can in principle change the propagating mode content. The honest statement is that the linearized theory does not obviously contain only healthy helicity-2 gravity plus gauge redundancy, and that the Partanen–Tulkki program needs an explicit unitary-spectrum demonstration in the physical branch. None has been published.

Barker's later work (arXiv:2406.12826, 2505.23894, 2506.02111, 2510.08201) develops the same spectral-analysis machinery on related theories but does not re-engage Partanen and Tulkki by name. To my knowledge, the 2023 paper remains the only independent technical critique of unified gravity in the spectrum-analysis line that has remained in the public discourse for two years without rebuttal.

## 6. Evidence structure across the four obstructions

The four obstructions are not at the same evidentiary level. Treating them as if they were would overstate the case. Treating only B as paper-internal would understate the cumulative weight. The table below separates them.

**Table 2. Evidence status for the four obstructions.**

| Obstruction | Type of evidence | Primary source(s) | Status on the present record |
|---|---|---|---|
| B (counterterm-basis mismatch) | Direct paper-internal equation reading | Partanen–Tulkki eqs. 157, 161, 162, 187–189, 194, 196, 203–207 | The published prescription is not Standard-Model-style BPHZ on the original local action. Strong. |
| A (matter-loop counterterms, broader sector) | Standard matter-coupled-gravity transfer; relevance argument | Duff 1994; Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen 1974; Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux 2000 | Strong as a prior expectation; the paper has not produced a cancellation theorem and §9.2 restricts to a small matter sector. |
| C (teleparallel non-closure) | Adjacent-literature transfer; teleparallel matter loops in general | Casadio–Kuntz–Paci 2022 | Highly relevant, not a direct calculation in unified-gravity variables. Strong by analogy. |
| D (free-field spectrum) | Limit-specific spectral analysis | Barker 2023 (arXiv:2311.11790) | Spectral warning in the weak-field zero-coupling limit; finite-coupling extensions are possible escape routes but unproven. |
| Goal (1) narrowness | Direct paper reading | Partanen–Tulkki §9.2 | The renormalization analysis covers electron-positron + electromagnetic + gravitational fields only, not the full Standard Model. Direct. |

The structural reading I argue for below is that A, B, and C are different expressions of the same closure failure — the bare action's parameter set is too small to absorb the divergences arising at one loop. D is different in kind: it concerns the propagating mode content of the linearized theory rather than the counterterm basis. The combined picture is that the strict one-loop renormalizability claim is not established on the present record, both because the paper's own subtraction is not BPHZ on the original local action (B) and because the matter-sector and teleparallel analyses suggest the bare parameter set would need enlargement to close (A, C), while the linearized theory's spectrum needs separate work (D).

I want to be careful about the status of this structural reading. It is not a formal theorem. The argument has three inputs, each with solid published backing but none an in-line calculation in Partanen–Tulkki's variables: (i) the explicit divergent-cocycle list in the standard 4D matter-coupled-gravity literature; (ii) the Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux cohomological classification of admissible counterterms; (iii) the paper-internal counterterm basis displayed in eqs. 157–205. From these, the natural inference is that every ghost-number-0 BRST cocycle occurring in the one-loop divergence must already have an independent coupling in the bare action (stability of the action under renormalization); the divergent cocycles in 4D matter-coupled gravity include $C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^2$, $R^2$, the torsion-quadratic invariants beyond $\mathcal{T}$, the torsion-quartic invariants, and the axial-current quartic; the bare action contains none of them; adding them enlarges the family; refusing to add them leaves the divergences uncanceled.

The structural reading is then: no local-BPHZ-renormalizable theory within the strict original family can absorb A, B, C, D simultaneously while preserving all three stated goals at original strength. At least one of (exact TEGR closure, two-derivative gravitational dynamics, finite-coupling renormalizability, healthy free spectrum, $U(1)^4$ field-space organization) must be abandoned. A counterexample would require either showing that some of the divergent cocycles vanish in this construction or showing that one of the four obstructions misreads it. I have looked for both and not found them. A direct refutation along either of those lines would materially alter the assessment given here.

## 7. The branch fork in the 2025 follow-up

The most consequential interpretive point arises from the July 2025 follow-up, arXiv:2507.07790, "Extending unified gravity to account for graviton-graviton interaction." That paper makes explicit a structural separation that the original paper did not clearly articulate.

Three direct quotes from arXiv:2507.07790 (verified against the html v2 of the preprint):

> There is no equivalence transformation from unified gravity to TEGR.

> The substitutions that produce TEGR break the gauge symmetry of unified gravity.

> The new gauge-invariant graviton-graviton self-interaction term does not contribute to the TEGR-reduced Lagrangian.

And on renormalization, the same paper closes with:

> Regarding the Feynman diagrams of UG, our extension introduces the triple-graviton vertex, whose implications to the renormalization of UG are left as a topic of further work.

Taken together, these statements strongly suggest a separation between two structurally distinct theories:

**The Minkowski-UG branch** preserves the four $U(1)$ gauge symmetry, contains the new triple-graviton vertex introduced in 2507.07790, and carries the 23.3% / 26.7% 2PN polarization-dependent light-deflection deviations predicted in arXiv:2505.14446 within this formulation. Its renormalization properties are unknown; the authors explicitly defer them. Its tree-level phenomenology is genuinely non-GR.

**The TEGR-reduced branch** is recovered from the Minkowski-UG branch by substitutions that break the gauge symmetry. It does not contain the new graviton-graviton self-interaction term in the same form. It classically agrees with GR.

The original paper's framing — that unified gravity reproduces GR via the teleparallel equivalence and also predicts new effects — is in tension with a reading in which the GR-reducing and non-GR-predictive formulations are equivalent presentations of a single gauge-invariant theory. The 23.3% / 26.7% deviations belong to the Minkowski-UG branch specifically. They are predictions of a theory connected to GR only through a substitution that breaks the gauge symmetry.

The fork also reframes the renormalization question. The original §9 analysis was carried out in the UGM formulation on a theory without graviton self-interaction. The 2025 follow-up admits that graviton self-interaction is a necessary extension; the renormalization analysis must therefore be restarted on the extended theory, which the authors acknowledge and defer.

## 8. Four coherent descendants

The strict claim is not established on the present record, but the program admits several coherent continuations. Four are structurally distinct, each abandoning at least one originally stated goal.

**Table 3. Four coherent descendants.**

| Descendant | Bare-action class | Preserves | Abandons | Main open problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1 Teleparallel / tetrad EFT | TEGR + New-GR + $C^2 + R^2 + G$ + torsion-quartic + $(J_5)^4$ + full local tower | Classical GR/TEGR limit; low-energy universal stress-tensor coupling | Strict perturbative renormalizability | Matching cutoff and operator coefficients to observation |
| 8.2 New-GR teleparallel | Three independent $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ torsion-quadratic couplings | Teleparallel structure; one-loop closure of the $T^2$ sector | Exact TEGR ratio $(1/4, 1/2, -1)$; goal (2) at original strength | Spectrum stability (Beltrán Jiménez–Heisenberg–Koivisto 2020 caveats) |
| 8.3 Higher-derivative curvature + fakeons | Stelle / Fradkin–Tseytlin $-\Lambda - (\bar M_P^2/2) R + \alpha(R_{\mu\nu}^2 - \tfrac13 R^2) - (\xi/6) R^2$; Anselmi–Piva fakeon prescription | Strict perturbative renormalizability | Exact TEGR closure; standard microcausality above fakeon scale | Phenomenology and the fakeon-scale measurement program |
| 8.4 Minkowski-UG as a distinct gauge theory | Gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG with the 2507.07790 triple-graviton vertex | Possibly all original gauge structure; the 2PN polarization prediction | Framing as a reformulation of GR | Healthy spectrum; counterterm closure for the extended theory; Ward identities; 2PN signature test |

The descendants merit a brief expansion.

### 8.1 Teleparallel / tetrad effective field theory

The most conservative option. Take the bare action to contain the full local operator basis allowed by symmetries: torsion-quadratic terms with independent coefficients $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$, torsion-quartic terms, curvature-squared terms, and the higher-dimensional fermionic operator tower including $(J_5^\mu J_{5\mu})^2$. Match the leading torsion-quadratic coefficients to the TEGR values $(1/4, 1/2, -1)$ at low energies. Treat higher-derivative terms as perturbative insertions, not resummed propagator poles. This is Donoghue–Burgess EFT of gravity (Donoghue 1994; Burgess 2004) in a teleparallel parameterization. It preserves the classical GR/TEGR limit and a low-energy form of the equivalence principle, abandoning strict perturbative renormalizability in the Yang–Mills sense.

### 8.2 New-GR teleparallel renormalization

Promote the three torsion-quadratic coefficients to independent renormalized parameters but stop short of including the full higher-derivative tower. This addresses obstruction C alone. It abandons exact TEGR closure but keeps teleparallel structure. Beltrán Jiménez, Heisenberg, and Koivisto (*Phys. Lett. B* **805**, 135422, 2020) showed that generic teleparallel quadratic theories around Minkowski typically contain extra symmetric rank-2 fields plus a 2-form, and the linear symmetries needed for viability can fail nonlinearly. New GR is a good leading EFT basis but not an established UV completion.

### 8.3 Higher-derivative curvature gravity with fakeons

Take the bare action in Stelle / Fradkin–Tseytlin form. Stelle (1977) proved renormalizability; Fradkin and Tseytlin (1982) proved asymptotic freedom of the essential couplings. The standard problem is a massive spin-2 ghost in the propagator. Anselmi and Piva (*JHEP* **05** (2018) 027; arXiv:1803.07777) showed that the fakeon prescription quantizes the massive spin-2 pole as a fakeon, preserving perturbative unitarity at the cost of modified microcausality above the fakeon scale. This option preserves goal (1) most strongly and abandons exact TEGR closure entirely; GR survives as an IR/classical limit only.

### 8.4 Minkowski-UG as a distinct gauge theory

The descendant the 2025 follow-ups are naturally read as motivating, without yet stating it explicitly. Drop the framing of GR equivalence and treat the gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG branch as a distinct theory of gravity on its own merits. The relevant benchmarks are then standard local-QFT health checks: a healthy particle spectrum around the relevant vacuum (addressing Barker); local counterterm-basis closure for the full theory including the triple-graviton vertex (addressing B and the 2025 deferral); viable Ward/Slavnov–Taylor identities; consistent EFT expansion; experimentally viable 2PN polarization predictions. Bensouilah's 2026 tree-level photon-photon scattering calculation in UGM, reproducing the classical Tolman–Ehrenfest–Podolsky description of the gravitational field produced by light, is a useful consistency check inside this descendant. The 2PN polarization signature is genuinely non-GR: in classical metric GR plus Maxwell theory, ray-optics light propagation is polarization-blind to leading order, and known polarization-dependent gravitational effects (spin-optics, helicity corrections, Drummond–Hathrell QED vacuum polarization in curved space) are sub-leading to ray optics, not 23% effects at second post-Newtonian order.

## 9. Note on related work

The Partanen–Tulkki series since the main paper consists of five tree-level follow-ups plus two corrigenda: the eight-spinor QED companion (*Phys. Rev. A* **109**, 032224, 2024; arXiv:2310.02285), light deflection (arXiv:2505.14446), perihelion precession (arXiv:2506.14447), atomic Dirac redshift (arXiv:2506.22057), the graviton-graviton extension (arXiv:2507.07790), and the corrigenda Rep. Prog. Phys. **88**, 069501 and **88**, 079501. The follow-ups stay at tree level; the July 2025 paper explicitly defers loop renormalization analysis of the extended theory. The recent follow-ups place increasing emphasis on the Minkowski-UG formulation.

Two independent technical critiques exist in the public record. Barker (arXiv:2311.11790) is the ghost-spectrum analysis covered in §5. A separate critique by Chishtie (*Chinese J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.cjph.2026.05.001) appears to exist as an article-in-press. The full text was paywall-blocked at time of writing and is not part of the present evidentiary basis.

Bensouilah (*Canadian J. Phys.* 2026, DOI 10.1139/cjp-2025-0362) provides a limited tree-level consistency check of single-graviton-exchange photon–photon scattering in the Minkowski-UG formulation, finding agreement with the classical Tolman–Ehrenfest–Podolsky description at small angles. The result does not bear on the one-loop closure issues discussed here.

A separate effective-field-theory unification program exists in the public record. Chishtie's USMEG-EFT framework (arXiv:2503.09796, *Canadian J. Phys.* accepted) reaches an EFT-based unification conclusion via different machinery, namely Lagrange-multiplier-restricted GR plus Appelquist–Carazzone decoupling, rather than teleparallel counterterm enlargement. USMEG-EFT is an independent EFT-style exit route, not corroboration of the equation-level obstructions in this comment. The honest framing is that two structurally similar exit routes have been reached by independent authors via different machinery; their conclusions are structurally aligned, their derivations are not the same.

The main contribution of this comment is to assemble the counterterm, teleparallel, spectral, and branch-structure issues into a single diagnostic of the published one-loop renormalizability claim, anchored on the equation-level paper-internal critique in §2 and the branch-fork reading of the 2025 follow-up in §7. The individual building blocks are not new. Their combination as applied to this specific construction, with paper-equation citations for obstruction B and verbatim quotes for the branch fork, is the contribution.

## 10. Conclusion

Partanen and Tulkki have written a serious paper. On my reading, the strict one-loop renormalizability claim is not established by the present technical record. The strongest evidence is paper-internal: the published subtraction prescription in eqs. 157–207 is not BPHZ closure of the original local action, and the matter-sector restriction in §9.2 means the demonstrated result was never a full Standard-Model-plus-gravity renormalization to begin with. Three further obstructions — matter-loop counterterms in the broader matter sector, teleparallel matter-loop divergences outside the TEGR family, and a ghost-mode free-field spectrum — sit at different evidentiary statuses (Table 2) and reinforce the same closure failure (A, C) or flag a separate spectral problem (D). The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2507.07790 makes explicit a branch fork between gauge-invariant Minkowski-UG and TEGR-reduced presentations that the original paper did not separate. Four coherent descendants remain (Table 3); each abandons at least one stated goal.

The framework remains a viable research direction if its branch choice and renormalization status are clarified. The most likely paths forward, if the authors want to preserve maximum continuity with the original construction, are descendant 8.1 (teleparallel EFT, abandoning strict renormalizability) or descendant 8.4 (Minkowski-UG as a distinct non-GR theory, abandoning the GR-equivalence framing). The recent follow-ups place increasing emphasis on the Minkowski-UG formulation.

On the present record, the strict one-loop renormalizability claim is not established. Results that would overturn this conclusion include: a demonstration that obstruction B misreads the paper's subtraction prescription, with explicit identification of either a local BPHZ-renormalizable bare action whose multiplicative renormalization actually closes on the displayed divergence or a symmetry-based cancellation theorem forbidding the offending counterterms; a local BPHZ-renormalizable bare action for the full nonlinear theory including the triple-graviton vertex; a spectrum analysis of the relevant vacuum showing only healthy modes, addressing Barker; or a direct rebuttal of the four standard escapes in §2 that identifies a fifth route I have not anticipated. Any of these would materially alter the assessment given here.

**Limitations.** I do not recalculate the one-loop integrals in unified-gravity variables. The critique concerns counterterm closure of the displayed expressions and their interpretation in the standard local-renormalization framework, together with the structural reading of the 2025 author follow-ups. A direct recomputation of the loop integrals in the original variables, with explicit display of the divergent tensor structure in the projector basis, would be valuable but is not attempted here.
