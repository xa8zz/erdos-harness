## Verdict: the claim is false

There is an explicit one-loop obstruction. The obstruction is not a BRST anomaly; it is worse for the claim: the Slavnov–Taylor/BRST symmetry **allows** a new local, gauge-invariant higher-derivative counterterm that is not present in the bare Lagrangian and cannot be absorbed by finite redefinitions of the original parameters.

The construction described in the problem matches the “unified gravity” setup of Partanen–Tulkki, *Gravity generated by four one-dimensional unitary gauge symmetries and the Standard Model*, arXiv:2310.01460, whose abstract states the four (U(1)) symmetries of a space-time dimension field, the eight-spinor formulation, the gauge-field-dependent TEGR/Weitzenböck condition, the gauge-field-independent Minkowski condition, and the asserted one-loop renormalizability. ([arXiv][1])

---

## 1. The dimensionless-coupling premise fails under canonical dimensions

Set (\hbar=c=1). In the TEGR-reduced sector, the gravitational kinetic term is not a Yang–Mills kinetic term with an ordinary fixed dimensionless coupling. In the candidate paper’s own notation, the gravity-gauge kinetic term is written with an Einstein-constant prefactor,
[
\mathcal L_{g,\mathrm{kin}}
===========================

\frac{1}{4\kappa} H^a_{\mu\nu} S_a^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g},
\qquad
\kappa=8\pi G,
]
and the same passage says this has the same mathematical form as TEGR. ([arXiv][2])

The later “dimensionless” form is obtained by the field rescaling
[
H_{a\nu}\mapsto \sqrt{\kappa},H'_{a\nu},
]
which removes (\kappa) from the quadratic kinetic prefactor, but moves (\sqrt{\kappa}) into the interaction strength. The paper then defines a “gravity fine-structure constant”
[
\alpha_g=\frac{G p^2}{\hbar c^3},
]
and explicitly says it depends on the energy scale through (p^2). ([arXiv][2]) ([arXiv][2])

That is the usual effective-field-theory expansion parameter (p^2/M_{\rm Pl}^2), not a fixed dimensionless BPHZ coupling like the Yang–Mills coupling. With standard 4D dimensions,
[
[A]=1,\qquad [\psi]=3/2,\qquad [T_{\mu\nu}]=4.
]
A universal gravity interaction has the schematic form
[
\mathcal L_{\rm int}\sim \sqrt{\kappa}, h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}.
]
Since ([h]=1), the coefficient (\sqrt{\kappa}) has mass dimension (-1). If one instead writes (g A^a_\mu T^\mu_a) with ([A]=1), then (g) must also have dimension (-1). A dimensionless (g) would make the interaction density dimension (5), not (4).

So the Yang–Mills power-counting premise is already broken.

---

## 2. The explicit one-loop counterterm

Because the TEGR geometric reduction is required to hold quantum mechanically, the theory must reproduce the weak-field coupling of a spin-2/tetrad perturbation to the matter stress tensor. The candidate paper itself states that, at the classical field-equation level, its TEGRW reduction agrees with conventional TEGR and hence with GR, since TEGR and GR actions differ by a boundary term. ([arXiv][1]) It also states that the graviton propagator obtained in the harmonic gauge equals the well-known linearized-GR graviton propagator. ([arXiv][2])

Now consider the one-loop two-graviton 1PI function generated by the spinor matter loop. Equivalently, compute the divergent part of the stress-tensor two-point function,
[
\langle T_{\mu\nu}(p)T_{\rho\sigma}(-p)\rangle_{\rm div}.
]
The local divergent term is the flat-space expansion of the Weyl-squared counterterm
[
\Delta\Gamma^{(1)}*{\rm div}
\supset
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
\frac{b}{(4\pi)^2}
\int d^4x,\sqrt{-g},
C*{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
]
Duff’s review, *Twenty years of the Weyl anomaly* (1994), records that a two-point graviton calculation about flat space fixes the (C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}) coefficient, and gives
[
b=\frac{N_s+6N_F+12N_V}{120}
]
in the usual ((4\pi)^{-2}) normalization, with all scalar, Dirac-fermion, and vector contributions positive. ([BPB US][3]) For one Dirac fermion, (b=1/20); for a Maxwell vector, (b=1/10). The exact normalization convention is not important here. The important fact is (b\neq0) for the stated spinor matter.

Pull this counterterm back to the TEGR/gauge field through the weak-field relation
[
g_{\mu\nu}
==========

\eta_{\mu\nu}
+
\sqrt{\kappa},h_{\mu\nu}[A]
+
O(\kappa).
]
Then
[
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
====================

\sqrt{\kappa},
C^{(1)}*{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[h[A]]
+
O(\kappa),
]
so
[
\Delta\Gamma^{(1)}*{\rm div}
\supset
\frac{\kappa b}{(4\pi)^2\epsilon}
\int d^4x,
C^{(1)}*{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[h[A]]
C^{(1)\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[h[A]]
+\cdots .
]
In (U(1)^4) gauge-field language this is schematically a four-derivative quadratic counterterm,
[
\Delta\mathcal L*{\rm div}
\sim
\frac{\kappa b}{(4\pi)^2\epsilon}
(\partial_\rho F^a_{\mu\nu})(\partial^\rho F_a{}^{\mu\nu})
+\text{index-projector terms},
]
where the omitted terms are the contractions that assemble the linearized Weyl projector.

Under the canonical dimensions in the problem,
[
[(\partial F)^2]=6.
]
The original kinetic term is (F^2), dimension (4), giving a (p^2) inverse propagator. The one-loop divergence above is a (p^4) inverse-propagator counterterm. Multiplicative field renormalization, mass renormalization, gauge-parameter renormalization, and coupling renormalization cannot turn a (p^2) kinetic term into a (p^4) term.

This is already enough to disprove one-loop BPHZ renormalizability.

## 3. The spinor sector gives an even stronger on-shell obstruction

There is also a classic spinor-specific obstruction. Deser and van Nieuwenhuizen, *Nonrenormalizability of the quantized Dirac-Einstein system*, Phys. Rev. D 10, 411 (1974), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.10.411, showed that the coupled Dirac–Einstein system is one-loop nonrenormalizable; the diagrams with eight external fermions yield a divergence proportional to the fourth power of the fermion axial-vector current. ([Physical Review Links][4])

That counterterm has the schematic form
[
\Delta\mathcal L_{\rm div}
\sim
\frac{1}{\epsilon},
\kappa^4
\bigl(\bar\psi\gamma^a\gamma_5\psi\bigr)^4
]
up to convention-dependent numerical and index factors. The axial current has dimension (3), so this is a dimension-(12) local operator before its powers of (\kappa). It is not a mass term, not a wave-function renormalization, not a Yukawa renormalization, and not a gauge-coupling renormalization.

This matters because spinors in a gravitational TEGR/Einstein reduction require tetrad/vierbein structure. The same Deser–van Nieuwenhuizen abstract notes that the gravitational variables required to couple gravity to fermions are vierbein fields with local Lorentz as well as coordinate invariance. ([Physical Review Links][4]) If the candidate theory avoids this divergence by not reproducing the Einstein–Dirac/TEGR spinor coupling, then it fails the required TEGR reduction with spinor matter. If it does reproduce that coupling, the divergence is present.

---

## 4. BRST does not remove the obstruction

For the abelian gauge fixing described in the problem,
[
s A^a_\mu=\partial_\mu c^a,\qquad sc^a=0.
]
Therefore
[
sF^a_{\mu\nu}=0,
]
and any local polynomial built from (F^a_{\mu\nu}), its derivatives, and the TEGR-soldered curvature tensors is BRST closed. The counterterm above is thus compatible with the Slavnov–Taylor identity; it is not forbidden by it.

Algebraic renormalization says precisely that admissible counterterms and possible anomalies are classified by local BRST cohomology. Barnich, Brandt, and Henneaux, *Local BRST cohomology in gauge theories*, Phys. Rep. 338 (2000) 439–569, arXiv:hep-th/0002245, review the computation of these cohomology groups and their use in counterterms and Wess–Zumino consistency conditions, including non-power-counting-renormalizable couplings. ([arXiv][5]) A gauge-invariant curvature-squared or ((\partial F)^2) term is a ghost-number-zero BRST-invariant counterterm, not a ghost-number-one anomaly. The Slavnov–Taylor identity therefore cannot be used to set its coefficient to zero.

So the failure mode is:

[
\boxed{
\text{BRST allows the divergence, but the original Lagrangian lacks its counterterm.}
}
]

That is the opposite of Yang–Mills renormalizability.

---

## 5. Why the Minkowski geometric condition does not save the claim

The alternative gauge-field-independent condition sets the metric to Minkowski for all (A). In the candidate paper, gauge invariance and gauge fixing are explicitly interpreted before the UGM geometric condition is applied, and the one-loop analysis is described as a restricted UGM/on-shell-renormalization-factor calculation. ([arXiv][1]) ([arXiv][1])

But the problem requires the TEGR geometric reduction (\eta=\eta(A)) to be respected at the perturbative quantum level. A calculation performed only after imposing the Minkowski condition does not renormalize the TEGR slice. It changes the off-shell theory. Once the TEGR relation is restored, the same one-loop stress-tensor divergence reappears as the (C^2) or ((\partial F)^2) counterterm.

Thus there are only two options:

1. **Add the new higher-derivative counterterm.** Then the bare Lagrangian has been enlarged beyond the original finite parameter set, so the claim is false.
2. **Forbid the counterterm to preserve the original Lagrangian.** Then the one-loop 1PI effective action remains divergent, so the claim is false.

---

## 6. Equivalence-principle mass renormalization is irrelevant

Requiring equality of renormalized inertial and gravitational masses is a renormalization condition on mass parameters. It cannot cancel a pure gravitational two-point divergence proportional to (C^2), nor the spinor-sector higher-dimensional divergence proportional to ((\bar\psi\gamma^a\gamma_5\psi)^4). These operators have different field content, derivative order, and canonical dimension from the mass terms.

---

# Final disproof

The theory is **not** one-loop renormalizable in the BPHZ/Yang–Mills sense. The unavoidable one-loop 1PI divergence contains at least the local counterterm
[
\boxed{
\Delta\Gamma^{(1)}*{\rm div}
\supset
\frac{\kappa b}{(4\pi)^2\epsilon}
\int d^4x,
C^{(1)}*{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[h[A]]
C^{(1)\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[h[A]]
}
]
equivalently, in canonical (U(1)^4) gauge-field variables, a dimension-six four-derivative term of the schematic form
[
\boxed{
\Delta\mathcal L_{\rm div}
\sim
\frac{\kappa b}{(4\pi)^2\epsilon}
(\partial F)^2 .
}
]

This term is Lorentz invariant, gauge invariant, and BRST closed. Its coefficient is nonzero for spinor matter. It is not contained in the original TEGR/Yang–Mills-like bare Lagrangian and cannot be absorbed by finite redefinitions of the original parameters. If the TEGR spinor coupling is enforced fully, the classic Einstein–Dirac one-loop eight-fermion divergence gives an additional nonrenormalizable obstruction.

Therefore the stated renormalizability claim is false.
