\section*{The prime theft-floor lemma}

Fix an integer $n \ge 16$. Let $Q$ denote the set of odd primes $q$ with
$3 \le q \le n/2$. Two players, $A$ and $B$, alternate moves with $A$
moving first, acting on a state consisting of a subset $L \subseteq Q$ of
\emph{live} primes, initially $L = Q$.

\begin{itemize}
\item On $A$'s move, $A$ chooses an integer $x$ with $2 \le x \le n$ and
removes from $L$ every prime that divides $x$. ($A$'s choice of $x$ is
otherwise unrestricted and may depend on the whole history.)
\item On $B$'s move, if $L \ne \emptyset$, $B$ removes the \emph{smallest}
element of $L$ and appends it to a list $F$ (we say $B$ \emph{scores} it).
\end{itemize}
The game ends when $L = \emptyset$.

\begin{lemma}[Theft floor]
There are absolute constants $c_0 > 0$ and $N_0$ such that for all
$n \ge N_0$ and every strategy of $A$,
\[
\sum_{q \in F} \frac{1}{q} \;\ge\; c_0 .
\]
Moreover one may take any $c_0 < \kappa/3$ for $n$ large, where $\kappa$
is any constant with $\theta(x) \ge \kappa x$ for all large $x$ (by the
prime number theorem, any $\kappa < 1$ works; unconditionally, explicit
Chebyshev bounds give e.g.\ $\kappa = 0.9$ for $x \ge 101$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\theta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \log p$ over primes $p$ (natural
logarithms throughout).

\emph{Step 1: the scored primes are small.}
Let $q^{(t)}$ be the prime scored on $B$'s $t$-th move. At that moment $A$
has made exactly $t$ moves, say with chosen integers $x_1, \dots, x_t \le n$.
Every prime $p < q^{(t)}$ is dead, hence either was scored by $B$ (at most
$t-1$ of these, each $\le n/2$) or was removed by some $A$-move. The primes
removed by the move $x_i$ all divide $x_i$, and distinct primes dividing
$x_i$ have $\sum \log p \le \log x_i \le \log n$. Therefore
\[
\theta\big(q^{(t)}{-}\big) \;=\; \sum_{p < q^{(t)}} \log p
\;\le\; t \log n + (t-1)\log (n/2) \;<\; 2t \log n .
\]
Fix $\kappa \in (0,1)$ and $x_\kappa$ with $\theta(x) \ge \kappa x$ for
$x \ge x_\kappa$. If $q^{(t)} \ge x_\kappa$ then, since
$\theta(q^{(t)}-) \ge \theta(q^{(t)}) - \log q^{(t)} \ge \kappa q^{(t)} - \log n$,
\[
q^{(t)} \;\le\; \frac{2t \log n + \log n}{\kappa} \;\le\; \frac{3t \log n}{\kappa}
\qquad (t \ge 1).
\]
The bound $q^{(t)} \le 3t\log n/\kappa$ also holds trivially when
$q^{(t)} < x_\kappa$ once $3\log n/\kappa \ge x_\kappa$, i.e.\ for all
$n \ge N_1(\kappa)$.

\emph{Step 2: the game is long.}
Each $A$-move removes at most $\log x/\log 3 \le \log n/\log 3$ primes
(distinct primes dividing $x$ are $\ge 3$), and each $B$-move removes one.
If $B$ has made $T$ scores in total, then counting all removals,
\[
|Q| \;\le\; (T+1)\Big(\frac{\log n}{\log 3}\Big) + T ,
\]
so, using $|Q| = \pi(n/2) - 1 \ge c_2\, n/\log n$ (Chebyshev),
\[
T \;\ge\; c_3\, \frac{n}{\log^2 n} \qquad (n \ge N_2).
\]

\emph{Step 3: harmonic sum.}
Combining,
\[
\sum_{q \in F} \frac1q \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{1}{q^{(t)}}
\;\ge\; \frac{\kappa}{3\log n} \sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac1t
\;\ge\; \frac{\kappa}{3}\cdot\frac{\log T}{\log n}
\;\ge\; \frac{\kappa}{3}\Big(1 - \frac{2\log\log n + C}{\log n}\Big),
\]
which exceeds any fixed $c_0 < \kappa/3$ for $n$ large. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Machine verification (exact adversarial simulation, $A$ = greedy
budget-packing minimizer, which dominates the tested alternatives): the
realized $\sum_{q \in F} 1/q$ equals $0.491,\ 0.537,\ 0.565,\ 0.589,\
0.616$ at $n = 10^4, \dots, 10^8$, and $\max_t q^{(t)}/(t\log n) \le 1.81$
throughout, consistent with the constants above.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Interpretation (not needed for the proof): in the divisibility-antichain
game on $\{2,\dots,n\}$, a play of $x$ kills exactly the live divisors and
multiples of $x$; hence any strategy of the maximizing player removes, per
move, only primes dividing a single integer $\le n$, and the minimizing
player may elect to play the smallest live prime. The lemma therefore gives
an unconditional positive lower bound, uniform over opponent strategies,
for the harmonic mass of small primes the minimizing player can fire, and
thus (via an upper-bound sieve) a positive fraction of top-half integers
killed through their smallest prime factors.
\end{remark}
