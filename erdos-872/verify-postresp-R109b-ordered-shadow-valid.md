# Verdict: **VALID**

Document B correctly refutes the literal statement (OS). Its construction gives, for every proposed \(H(n)\to\infty\), a genuinely obtainable post-Phase-0 position \(A_0\) such that every ordering of \(M_0\) admits a legal Prolonger continuation with a linear raw proper-multiple shadow. The private-carrier drafting argument, the arithmetic constructions, and the quantifier reversal all survive scrutiny. 

There are two harmless wording imprecisions in the prime regime, noted below, but neither creates a logical gap.

## 1. Obtainability: **passes**

Let

\[
Y=\left\lfloor\frac nH\right\rfloor,\qquad
S_Y=\{\lfloor Y/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,Y\}.
\]

The proposed Phase-0 history is legal:

1. \(S_Y\) is an antichain. If \(a<b\) lie in \(S_Y\), then \(b\le Y<2a\), so \(a\nmid b\).
2. Therefore, after any subset of \(S_Y\) has been played, every unplayed member of \(S_Y\) remains legal.
3. Every Shortener move chosen from \(S_Y\) conforms to Phase 0, because it is a currently legal integer at most \(Y\le n/H\).
4. Prolonger may legally choose the alternating members of \(S_Y\).

Once all of \(S_Y\) is selected, every \(2\le d\le Y\) is illegal. For \(d\le Y/2\),

\[
\left\lfloor\frac Yd\right\rfloor d\in S_Y
\]

is a selected multiple of \(d\); for \(d>Y/2\), \(d\in S_Y\) itself.

The parity can be made completely explicit. Writing \(s=|S_Y|\):

- If \(s\) is odd, alternating selections of \(S_Y\), beginning with Prolonger, end with a Prolonger move. Shortener then begins the sweep, and \(A_0=S_Y\).
- If \(s\) is even, they end with a Shortener move. Prolonger has one intervening turn before Shortener’s next turn and may play an upper-half prime \(q_n\in(n/2,n]\), giving
  \[
  A_0=S_Y\cup\{q_n\}.
  \]

When \(Y<2\), Prolonger’s opening upper-half prime gives the stated \(A_0\).

The construction does require Shortener to choose particular permitted Phase-0 moves, but that is **not** a defect in the refutation. The literal (OS) says “for every \(A_0\) obtainable after Phase 0.” Thus one reachable Phase-0 history is enough to falsify it. A different statement concerning only the position generated by some fixed, specially chosen Phase-0 tie-breaking policy would have different quantifiers, but that is not (OS).

## 2. Membership in the first minimal layer: **passes**

### Rough roots

For \(m\) with

\[
Y<m\le \frac n5,\qquad P^-(m)>h,
\]

every proper divisor \(d\mid m\) satisfies

\[
d\le \frac{m}{P^-(m)}
<\frac mh
\le\frac{n}{5h}
<\frac Y2
\]

for large \(n\). Hence every proper divisor of \(m\) is already illegal at \(A_0\).

Moreover, no \(s\in S_Y\) divides \(m\), since such an \(s\) would be a proper divisor exceeding \(Y/2\), contradicting the displayed bound. Conversely \(m>Y\ge s\), so \(m\nmid s\). The optional upper-half prime is also incomparable with \(m<n/2\).

Thus \(m\) is legal and has no legal proper divisor, so \(m\in M_0\).

### Prime roots

In the \(Y\ge4\) prime construction, each

\[
p>2n^{3/4}\ge Y
\]

is a prime exceeding all of \(S_Y\). Its only proper divisor is \(1\), outside the board, so \(p\) is legal and minimal.

When \(Y<4\), the primes \(p\in(n^{1/4},n^{1/3}]\) similarly exceed \(S_Y\), and again have no nontrivial proper divisor.

Therefore all claimed roots really are in the **first** minimal layer, not merely in some later legal layer.

## 3. Private carriers and their later legality: **passes**

### Rough regime

Because \(P^-(m)>h>2\), each \(m\) is odd. The divisors of \(2m\) are \(d\) and \(2d\), where \(d\mid m\). Other than \(m\) and \(2m\), every such divisor is at most

\[
\frac{2m}{P^-(m)}
<\frac{2n}{5h}
<\frac Y2.
\]

Consequently no member of \(S_Y\) divides \(2m\), while \(2m>Y\) prevents \(2m\) from dividing a member of \(S_Y\). Hence \(2m\) is legal at \(A_0\).

Among elements of \(M_0\), the only divisor of \(2m\) is \(m\): every other proper divisor is already illegal, and \(2m\notin M_0\) because it has the legal divisor \(m\).

The carriers are pairwise incomparable. If \(2m\mid2m'\), then \(m\mid m'\). For \(m\ne m'\), the quotient \(m'/m\) is a nontrivial divisor of the \(h\)-rough number \(m'\), so it exceeds \(h\). But simultaneously

\[
\frac{m'}m<\frac{n/5}{n/h}=\frac h5,
\]

a contradiction.

### Prime regimes

For \(Y\ge4\), the divisors of \(2p\) are \(1,2,p,2p\). No selected member of \(S_Y\) is \(2\), and every member of \(S_Y\) is smaller than \(p\). Therefore no selected element divides \(2p\), so \(2p\) is legal and has trace exactly \(\{p\}\) on \(M_0\).

Strictly speaking, the sentence “\(2\) is already illegal, hence \(2p\) is legal” would not by itself be sufficient: the move making \(2\) illegal might conceivably be comparable with \(2p\). Here, however, the complete divisor list of \(2p\) supplies the required direct verification, so there is no gap.

For \(Y<4\), \(p^2\) has only the nontrivial proper divisor \(p\). It is not divisible by any selected \(2\) or \(3\), and distinct prime squares are incomparable.

Document B also says in this case that the primes \(p\) “exceed every member of the post-peel position.” That is false when the optional upper-half prime \(q_n\) is present. The correct statement is that \(p\) exceeds every member of \(S_Y\), while \(p,p^2\) are incomparable with \(q_n\). Document B states the latter immediately afterward, so the false wording is unused.

### Accumulation during the sweep

The private trace condition and pairwise incomparability are exactly what is needed:

- A previously selected root \(r\in M_0\) cannot divide \(c_v\), unless \(r=v\).
- The surviving condition excludes \(r=v\).
- If \(c_v\mid r\), then \(v\mid r\), contradicting incomparability of distinct elements of \(M_0\).
- Earlier carriers are incomparable with \(c_v\).

Thus every designated carrier remains legal when Prolonger needs it.

## 4. Drafting lemma and alternation: **passes**

The alternation accounting is correct.

Under the private-carrier strategy, every root of \(K\) has exactly two possible outcomes:

- Prolonger plays its carrier, placing it in \(B\);
- Shortener selects the root exactly, placing it in \(T\).

No Shortener-selected root can disable another designated carrier, by the private trace property. Thus Shortener cannot burn carriers faster than one per turn.

Since Shortener begins the first sweep in the concrete Phase-0 history, at most her first selected root is unpaired. Every later \(t\in T\) has an immediately preceding Prolonger turn, on which Prolonger chose some distinct \(v(t)\in B\). At that earlier moment \(t\) was still available, so greedy choice gives

\[
\Delta_{v(t)}(B_t)\ge \Delta_t(B_t).
\]

When \(t\) is subsequently added to the final \(B\) together with the earlier members of \(T\), its base set is a superset of \(B_t\). Submodularity therefore gives

\[
\Delta_t(B\cup T_{<t})\le \Delta_t(B_t)
\le \Delta_{v(t)}(B_t).
\]

Distinct \(t\)’s are charged to distinct entry marginals of \(B\). Their total is at most \(F(B)\), while the one possible initial unpaired root contributes at most \(b\). Hence

\[
F(K)-F(B)\le F(B)+b,
\]

which yields

\[
F(B)\ge \frac{F(K)-b}{2}.
\]

Shortener may indeed make many elements of the raw shadow illegal with her own sweep moves. That does **not** invalidate this lemma because the function in (OS) counts the raw set

\[
\bigcup_{m\in K_0}\{2m,3m,\ldots\},
\]

not the subset still legal at sweep end.

## 5. Arithmetic estimates: **pass**

### Rough-number construction

The stated sieve estimate is appropriate uniformly for \(X\ge h^3\). With level

\[
D=\frac{X}{(\log X)^4},
\]

one has

\[
\frac{\log D}{\log h}\ge 3-o(1),
\]

and the total \(O(1)\)-per-divisor remainder over the Rosser support is \(O(D)=o(X/\log h)\). Thus

\[
\#\{X<m\le2X:P^-(m)>h\}\asymp\frac{X}{\log h}
\]

with absolute constants.

There are \(\Theta(\log h)\) admissible dyadic intervals between \(2Y\) and \(n/5\). Each contributes \(\gg1/\log h\) to the reciprocal sum, so

\[
\sum_{m\in K}\frac1m\ge\eta
\]

for an absolute \(\eta>0\). Conversely,

\[
|K|=O\!\left(\frac n{\log h}\right)=o(n).
\]

The shadow disjointness is also correct. In an equality

\[
km=k'm',
\qquad k,k'<h/2,
\]

all prime factors of \(k,k'\) are below \(h\), while every prime factor of \(m,m'\) exceeds \(h\). Uniqueness of factorization separates the two parts, giving \(k=k'\) and \(m=m'\).

Therefore

\[
F(K)
=\sum_{m\in K}
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2m}\right\rfloor-1\right)
\ge c_1n.
\]

The singleton bound \(b<h/2=o(n)\) is valid in the regime \(h<n^{1/4}\).

### Prime constructions

The reciprocal sums are correct:

\[
\sum_{2n^{3/4}<p\le n/4}\frac1p
=
\log\frac43+o(1),
\]

and

\[
\sum_{n^{1/4}<p\le n^{1/3}}\frac1p
=
\log\frac43+o(1).
\]

Since \(\log(4/3)>1/10\), a family with total reciprocal mass

\[
s=\frac1{10}+o(1)
\]

exists. The assertion \(|K|=o(n)\) uses the standard consequence \(\pi(x)=o(x)\); this is omitted in the prose but is not a substantive gap and follows from the same standard prime-distribution input being invoked.

For distinct primes,

\[
|U_p\cap U_q|=\left\lfloor\frac N{pq}\right\rfloor.
\]

Thus second-order Bonferroni gives

\[
\left|\bigcup U_p\right|
\ge Ns-|K|-\frac N2s^2.
\]

Removing the distinct singleton primes costs another \(|K|\), yielding

\[
F(K)\ge
N\left(s-\frac{s^2}{2}\right)-2|K|
\ge c_2n.
\]

Finally, the maximum singleton shadow is \(O(n^{1/4})\) in the \(2p\) case and \(O(n^{3/4})\) in the \(p^2\) case, hence \(o(n)\).

## 6. Shadow accounting and quantifiers: **pass**

In the rough regime, the shadows are genuinely disjoint, so their weights add exactly. In the prime regime, Bonferroni supplies the required linear union lower bound despite overlaps.

The drafting lemma produces \(B\subseteq K_0\), so monotonicity of coverage gives

\[
|\operatorname{Sh}^-(K_0)|\ge F(B)\ge\varepsilon n.
\]

The quantifier reversal is exact. Abstracting the asymptotic notation, (OS) has the form

\[
\exists H\to\infty\;
\forall A_0\;
\exists\prec\;
\forall\text{ continuations}\;[\text{shadow}=o(n)].
\]

Document B establishes the stronger negation

\[
\forall H\to\infty\;
\exists A_0\;
\forall\prec\;
\exists\text{ continuation}\;
[\text{shadow}\ge\varepsilon n]
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\), with one absolute \(\varepsilon>0\).

Since

\[
|\operatorname{Sh}^-(K_0)|
\le
\sum_{m\in K_0}
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2m}\right\rfloor-1\right),
\]

the same example also refutes WOS.

## 7. Scope of the refutation: **Document B is correct**

The construction refutes only the raw-shadow conditions OS and WOS. It does **not** refute the cumulative minimal-layer statement.

The containment theorem says

\[
\bigcup_{\ell\ge1}M_\ell
\subseteq \operatorname{Sh}^-(K_0).
\]

This is an upper containment, not an equivalence or lower bound. A linear right-hand side says nothing by itself about how much of that shadow remains legal.

Indeed:

- In the rough and \(2p\) constructions, playing \(2m\) immediately blocks every even multiple of \(m\).
- Shortener’s exact selections of other first-layer roots can block shadow elements having those roots as additional divisors.
- In the \(Y<4\) prime-square example, most multiples of a killed prime may also have some other prime divisor that Shortener selects exactly during the same sweep.

Thus a linear raw shadow can coexist with a much smaller surviving descendant set. Additional chronological control across later layers would be required to turn this construction into a refutation of growing-depth uniformity.

So Document B’s own scope assessment is accurate: **OS and WOS are false, while the cumulative-layer problem remains open under the supplied facts.**
