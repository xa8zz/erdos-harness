# Verdict

I do **not** have a valid proof of either forced entry or inevitable failure.

The computations favor entry, but the asymptotic step is not a numerical one. After reducing away ordinary lift failures and pairwise cross-carrier effects, the problem becomes an evolving **weighted blocker game** on the divisor lattice of a primorial. I can solve the frozen one-spoiler case and several structured multi-spoiler cases, including the diagonal semiprime matching. I cannot prove the required time-area inequality for a general blocker clutter generated online by maximum-degree replies, nor can I construct a maximum-degree trajectory violating it while keeping the exact-move transfer sublinear.

That is the single remaining sub-step.

The manuscript’s static sparse-cover, completion, trapping, and regeneration results are used as supplied.

# 1. Moderate-(n) entry-race experiments

I represented the position by the exact divisibility graph between legal lower moves and live upper targets. The tested Prolonger rule was:

[
u_i\in\arg\max_{u\in R_i}
#\left{
d\in D_i:
d\le \frac nH,\ d\mid u
\right}.
\tag{1}
]

Thus Prolonger always chooses a live upper target which batches the largest number of currently dangerous lower moves. Shortener then plays a true maximum-current-degree lower move, with the smallest integer used to break ties.

Entry was declared at the first state satisfying (\Delta\le H).

| (n) | (H) | rounds to entry | ( |R|/n) at entry | (\sum\Delta_i/n) from Shortener |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5,000 | 8 | 28 | 0.2922 | 0.2022 |
| 10,000 | 16 | 27 | 0.3072 | 0.1901 |
| 50,000 | 64 | 27 | 0.3252 | 0.1743 |
| 100,000 | 128 | 27.5 | 0.3372 | 0.1625 |
| 200,000 | 256 | 24.5 | 0.3501 | 0.1497 |

The first carrier in these runs is highly composite. For example, at (n=50{,}000), (H=256), the relevant beginning was

[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\text{Prolonger} &27720&40800&29808&27528&48504&37524&25728\
\text{Shortener} &13&19&29&41&49&61&71
\end{array}
]

with Shortener degrees

[
1923,\ 1215,\ 754,\ 515,\ 420,\ 331,\ 277.
]

The highly composite moves rapidly force the maximum-degree reply upward.

A deliberately low-degree, one-step “cross-spoil as much future batching as possible” heuristic behaved in the opposite way: it took more rounds but killed substantially less reservoir. At (n=1000,H=16), the maximum-degree response reached entry in approximately three rounds with (0.394n) reservoir remaining, whereas the cross-spoiling heuristic used about (5.5) rounds and left (0.451n).

So the finite experiment displays exactly the hoped-for dichotomy:

[
\text{large-degree reply}\Longrightarrow\text{area spent},
\qquad
\text{cross-spoiling reply}\Longrightarrow\text{time spent}.
\tag{2}
]

But these (H)'s are much too large relative to the tested (n) to establish the required (H=n^{o(1)}) regime. The simulations do not control the accumulation over the many degree shells that appear asymptotically.

# 2. A completely robust repair rule that is quantitatively too expensive

There is one simple way to eliminate cross-spoiling altogether.

For a position (A), put

[
D_H(A)={d\in D(A):\deg_A(d)>H}.
]

## Chain-repair lemma

Let (\mathcal C) be any divisibility chain of lower integers. On one Prolonger move, all currently dangerous elements of (\mathcal C) can be permanently removed.

### Proof

If (\mathcal C\cap D_H(A)=\varnothing), nothing is required. Otherwise let (d) be the divisibility-largest member of (\mathcal C\cap D_H(A)).

Because (\deg_A(d)>H\ge0), some (u\in R(A)) satisfies (d\mid u). Prolonger plays such a (u). Every dangerous (e\in\mathcal C) with (e\le d) divides (d), hence

[
e\mid d\mid u,
]

and becomes illegal. Every member above (d) was already non-dangerous by the choice of (d), and degrees can only decrease later.

Thus the chain can never again contain a dangerous lower move. ∎

A Shortener move belonging to the chain is even stronger: since every two elements of a chain are comparable, it makes the entire chain illegal.

This gives a fully online repair scheme against arbitrary opposition. Its cost is the number of chains needed to cover the dangerous roots. Unfortunately, it destroys the batching gain. For example,

[
\left(\frac{x}{2},x\right]
]

is an antichain under divisibility, so any chain partition of ([1,x]) has at least (x/2) chains. In the degree-(H) boundary layer this is already (\Theta(n/H)).

The lemma is useful diagnostically:

> Cross-spoiling itself is not an issue if one is willing to pay one move per chain. The entire problem is whether the Boolean faces inside a primorial batch can be repaired substantially more cheaply than their width, while keeping Shortener’s cumulative fresh area small.

# 3. The exact internal blocker game

Fix a squarefree primorial

[
Q=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p.
]

For clarity, first consider a (Q)-coprime stripped core (g). The same description works in general after absorbing mandatory small-prime copies into the base.

A root in the (g)-cell has the form

[
d(S)=g\prod_{p\in S}p,
\qquad S\subseteq\mathcal P,
\tag{3}
]

subject to the numerical cutoff (d(S)\le x).

Suppose a Shortener move internal to this cell has support (A\subseteq\mathcal P). A carrier pattern with included-prime set (T\subseteq\mathcal P) is divisible by that Shortener move exactly when

[
A\subseteq T.
]

Write

[
Z=\mathcal P\setminus T
]

for the primes omitted by the carrier. Then the carrier is legal relative to every previous internal spoiler (A\in\mathscr A) exactly when

[
Z\cap A\ne\varnothing
\qquad\text{for every }A\in\mathscr A.
\tag{4}
]

In other words, (Z) must be a transversal of the spoiler clutter (\mathscr A).

The carrier covers the root (S) exactly when

[
S\subseteq T,
]

equivalently

[
S\cap Z=\varnothing.
\tag{5}
]

Consequently, after Prolonger has used omission sets (Z_1,\ldots,Z_j), every surviving root support must satisfy

[
S\cap Z_\ell\ne\varnothing
\qquad(1\le\ell\le j).
\tag{6}
]

At the same time it must remain incomparable with every Shortener support:

[
S\nsubseteq A,\qquad A\nsubseteq S
\qquad(A\in\mathscr A).
\tag{7}
]

Thus the unresolved dynamics inside one batch are exactly:

1. Prolonger chooses a transversal (Z_j) of the current spoiler clutter.
2. Every live support disjoint from (Z_j) is cleared.
3. Shortener chooses a surviving support of maximum current star degree and inserts it into the clutter.
4. The new support may invalidate many previously planned transversals.

In a narrow divisor shell, maximum degree is, up to constant factors and prior target loss, the same as minimum numerical product

[
\prod_{p\in S}p.
]

So this is a **weighted online blocker game**: Prolonger inserts transversals, while Shortener inserts minimum-product sets hitting all earlier transversals.

This reduction exposes why a static primorial carrier is so powerful and why refreshing it is difficult. A static carrier uses (Z=\varnothing). Once even one spoiler is present, every legal refresh must omit a hitting set of the spoiler family.

# 4. A frozen single spoiler is not the obstruction

The one-spoiler problem has an exact solution.

## Frozen one-spoiler lemma

Suppose the spoiler clutter consists of one nonempty support

[
A={q_1,\ldots,q_r},
]

and no further spoiler is inserted during the repair. Then (r) carrier patterns clear every root incomparable with (A).

Moreover, ordering

[
q_1>q_2>\cdots>q_r
]

gives product escalation: after the first (j) repair carriers, every surviving support is divisible by

[
q_1q_2\cdots q_j.
]

### Proof

Use the singleton omission patterns

[
Z_j={q_j}.
]

Each (Z_j) hits (A), so its carrier is legal relative to the frozen spoiler.

A root survives the (j)-th carrier only if it intersects (Z_j), meaning (q_j\in S). Hence after (j) such carriers,

[
{q_1,\ldots,q_j}\subseteq S.
]

After all (r), every survivor would contain (A), contradicting its required incomparability with the spoiler (A). ∎

If a minimum-product reply is allowed between these frozen repair steps but does not add another constraint to this cell, the normalized reciprocal weight of those replies is bounded by

[
\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}
\frac1{q_1q_2\cdots q_j}
\le
\frac2{q_1}.
\tag{8}
]

The estimate follows because every subsequent prime factor is at least (2).

So a single middle-rank cross-spoiler does not by itself defeat primorial batching. The failure is that every actual Shortener reply adds a new edge to the clutter. The next singleton ({q_j}) need not hit that new edge, and therefore its intended repair carrier may already be illegal.

# 5. Even the diagonal semiprime obstruction has high-order repairs

The quadratic family

[
p_iq_j,\qquad i\ne j,
]

looks disastrous to a pair charge, but it is not by itself a proof of the no-direction.

Suppose the frozen spoiler clutter is a matching

[
\mathscr A
==========

\bigl{
{p_i,q_i}:1\le i\le t
\bigr}.
\tag{9}
]

A legal carrier pattern may contain at most one of (p_i,q_i) for every (i). Represent such a maximal pattern by a binary word

[
\sigma\in{0,1}^t,
]

where (\sigma_i=0) means that the carrier contains (p_i), and (\sigma_i=1) means it contains (q_i).

A cross-root involving at most (r) pairs specifies at most (r) coordinates of this binary word.

## Covering-array lemma

There is a family of

[
M=O!\left(2^r r\log(2t)\right)
\tag{10}
]

legal carrier patterns which covers every surviving cross-root involving at most (r) pairs.

### Proof

Choose (M) independent uniformly random binary words.

A prescribed partial assignment on at most (r) coordinates is extended by one random word with probability at least (2^{-r}). Thus the probability it is missed by all (M) words is at most

[
e^{-M/2^r}.
]

The number of partial assignments on at most (r) coordinates is at most

[
\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom tk2^k
\le (2et)^r.
]

Taking

[
M\ge 2^r\bigl(r\log(2et)+1\bigr)
]

makes the union-bound failure probability less than one. Hence a deterministic family exists. ∎

For (r=2), all quadratic cross-products are therefore covered by only

[
O(\log t)
]

carrier patterns in the idealized primorial cell.

This matters conceptually:

[
\boxed{
\text{Quadratic regeneration does not itself establish the no-direction.}
}
]

The pair charge is quadratically weak because it refuses to use higher-order carriers. Binary-code carriers can simultaneously resolve many cross-products.

On the other hand, covering all ranks requires (r) to grow. The pattern count (2^r r\log t), the numerical product of the carrier, and the appearance of new online spoiler edges then interact. That is where the proof stops.

# 6. Why these repairs do not yet force entry

There are three distinct levels.

### Frozen clutter

For a fixed spoiler family, one can use minimal transversals, singleton repairs, chain decompositions, or covering arrays. This is a static covering problem.

### Evolving clutter

After every carrier move, maximum-degree Shortener inserts a new edge. A transversal legal one round earlier may cease to be legal. The family of minimal transversals can undergo an exponential change after one new edge.

### Ordered multi-cell game

A lower move with stripped core (h) may spoil cells whose core (g) is a multiple of (h). Thus an edge inserted while processing one cell can alter many future cells. The supplied pair charge controls this only at rank two. It does not control the higher-order minimal transversals created by repeated cross-spoiling.

The key numerical tradeoff is not merely the number of transversals. A large repair family is favorable to Prolonger if executing it already consumes (\Omega(X/h)) rounds. Conversely, a small number of high-degree replies may spend (\Omega(X)) reservoir area before that many rounds occur.

Therefore neither of the following is sufficient:

[
\text{“There are exponentially many repairs,”}
]

because that can itself prove a long game, or

[
\text{“Each reply has large degree,”}
]

because Prolonger may preempt many such replies with one coded carrier.

The proof must compare **repair time and fresh area in the same inequality**.

# 7. The exact missing time-area inequality

Here is the precise statement I cannot prove.

Work in one dyadic target shell (S_X), after removing the exact-only bank. Fix a batching parameter (h\to\infty). Run an adaptive primorial scheduler:

* each ordinary batch contributes one carrier task;
* an internal spoiler replaces a task by legal transversal-repair tasks;
* external lift failures and ancestor interactions use the already available shell-stable pair charge;
* (N_{\mathrm{att}}(t)) is the number of carrier tasks actually attempted through time (t);
* (\Delta_i) is the fresh non-exact shell area killed by the (i)-th maximum-degree Shortener reply.

The needed high-order blocker inequality is

[
\boxed{
\sum_{i<t}\Delta_i
\le
\varepsilon(h)X
+
\varepsilon(h),h,N_{\mathrm{att}}(t),
\qquad
\varepsilon(h)\longrightarrow0,
}
\tag{TA}
]

uniformly until the shell is cleared or (N_{\mathrm{att}}(t)\asymp X/h).

Exact-only transfers are omitted from the left side and paid from the exact-move bank.

## Why (TA) forces entry

Run the scheduler until one of two events occurs.

If

[
N_{\mathrm{att}}\ge cX/h,
]

then the game has already lasted (\Omega(X/h)) rounds.

Otherwise

[
N_{\mathrm{att}}<cX/h,
]

and (TA) gives

[
\sum_i\Delta_i
\le
\varepsilon(h)X
+
c\varepsilon(h)X
=o(X).
]

The carrier moves themselves consume only (O(X/h)=o(X)) targets, and the exact-only transfers are banked. Hence a linear fraction of the shell survives when the batching process reaches its degree cap. The absorbing-fortress theorem then supplies another (\Omega(X/h)) moves.

Taking a linear shell and (h=n^{o(1)}) would yield

[
L(n)\ge \frac{n}{n^{o(1)}}=n^{1-o(1)}.
]

## What would prove the opposite direction

For the no-direction one needs an actual maximum-degree counterstrategy showing that every legal transversal scheduler satisfies, before entry,

[
\sum_{i<t}\Delta_i
\ge cX-o(X)
\tag{11}
]

while

[
t=o(X/h)
]

and only (o(X)) targets enter the exact bank.

That would be the ordered-area obstruction required by (OA), and hence would imply (L(n)=o(n)).

# 8. Why the present tools stop exactly at (TA)

The current pair charge can certify roughly one unit for a pair of carriers. In the blocker model this means it controls intersections involving two spoiler edges. But a legal carrier omission is a transversal of the **entire** spoiler clutter. Its rank is not bounded.

The frozen single-edge lemma proves (TA)-type behavior at blocker rank one.

The shell-stable pair charge treats rank two, but loses quadratically.

The covering-array lemma shows that a matching of rank-two spoilers can nevertheless have efficient high-order repairs. So the quadratic loss cannot simply be declared real.

What is missing is a rank-uniform statement of one of these two forms:

[
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{Persistence:}\quad
\text{high-order transversals reduce fresh area to }o(h)
\text{ per repair task};[2mm]
&\textbf{No-direction:}\quad
\text{maximum-degree replies force }\Omega(h)
\text{ fresh area per repair task}
\text{ for }\Omega(X/h)\text{ tasks}.
\end{aligned}
]

Neither follows from the number of minimal transversals, from the pair charge, from static completion, or from the exact-only bank.

# Final status

The moderate-(n) race looks favorable to Prolonger, and two apparent obstructions can be removed:

1. A frozen middle-rank spoiler has a product-escalating repair.
2. The diagonal semiprime matching has logarithmic coded repairs for all rank-two cross-products.

But those are frozen-clutter statements. The actual problem continually inserts new minimum-product edges into the blocker clutter.

Therefore I cannot validly claim either (a) or (b). The single unresolved sub-step is:

[
\boxed{
\text{Prove or refute the high-order time-area inequality ((TA))
for the evolving weighted blocker clutter generated by actual
maximum-degree replies.}
}
]

A proof of ((TA)) gives robust entry and, with the fortress theorem, an (n^{1-o(1)}) lower bound. A maximum-degree construction forcing the reverse inequality with negligible exact transfer gives the online-area no-direction and hence (L(n)=o(n)).
