Setting. Two players alternately choose previously unchosen integers from {2,...,n}, always keeping the chosen set an antichain under divisibility. The game ends when the set is maximal. An integer is "live" at a position if it is incomparable under divisibility to every chosen integer (hence still legally playable). Playing x removes from the live set x itself and every live divisor and multiple of x. "Burning" an integer d means playing a proper multiple of d while d is still live (d becomes illegal without being played).

Claim 1 (live-prime charge). At any position where every live element has at most D live comparables, the number of live elements having at least one live prime factor is at most (D+1)*pi(n). Argument: map each such element x to a live prime p dividing x; then x = p or x is a live proper multiple of p; each live prime p has at most D live comparables, so at most D live proper multiples; hence each of the at most pi(n) live primes receives at most D+1 elements.
