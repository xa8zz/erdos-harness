Don't search the internet. This is a test to see how well you can craft non-trivial, novel and creative proofs given a "primitive sets and divisibility games" math problem. Provide a full unconditional proof or disproof of the problem.

## Problem

For n >= 2, two players alternately choose previously unchosen integers from {2,...,n}, always keeping the chosen set an antichain under divisibility (a primitive set). The game ends when the set is maximal (no further integer can be added while keeping it a primitive set). Prolonger moves first and maximizes the total number of moves; Shortener minimizes it; L(n) is the length under optimal play by both sides. Prove or disprove: L(n) = o(n).

## Known progress

Complete proofs exist for all items unless marked otherwise; take them as given. General notation: for a position A, write U_n = (n/2, n] and L_n = [2, n/2]; R(A) is the subset of U_n still legal relative to A, D(A) the subset of L_n still legal; deg_A(d) = #{u in R(A) : d | u}; Delta(A) = max_d deg_A(d).

- Bounds: c_delta * n(log log n)^2/log n <= L(n) <= (0.1897123371 + o(1))n, both by explicit strategies.
  Lower-bound method: for every fixed 0 < delta < 1/4, an explicit weighted-activation graph on rank-three "upper-half fan" targets -- semiprimes 2ab in (n/2, n] with no proper multiple <= n, hence attackable only through their proper divisors -- reduces to a tight Maker-Breaker edge race on the (a,b) pairs; explicit max-threat selectors make the resulting game-length potential nondecreasing, with no restricted safe-edge hypothesis needed.
  Upper-bound method: a labelled odd-part comparison -- the map x -> (omega(x), nu_2(x)) is injective on any primitive set A, and if x, y in A with omega(x) | omega(y) then necessarily nu_2(x) > nu_2(y), so A embeds into an antichain of the labelled poset (u,a) <= (v,b) iff u | v and a <= b. (This repairs an earlier false claim that the plain odd parts of a primitive set form a primitive set: {12, 30} is primitive but its odd parts 3, 15 satisfy 3 | 15; the labelled version survives because nu_2(12) = 2 > 1 = nu_2(30).) Combined with a 4th-order Bonferroni inequality applied via monotone replacement (error o(n)) and a prime-rounding bridge transferring an idealized bin construction to actual primes (sup p_j/b_j = 1 + o(1)), chaining gives L(n) <= (W_4/2 + o(1))n with W_4/2 = 0.1897123371.

- Downward-shadow dichotomy (four-layer causal chronology): for a Shortener reply p_j discharging a preparation-obligation set O_j, and any nonempty U subset of O_j with d_U = gcd{c_e : e in U} >= 2, exactly one of the following holds: d_U is legal at Shortener position j, forcing |U| <= Delta_j (the current maximum degree); or d_U is already illegal at j, with an earlier first Prolonger blocker p_i (i < j) satisfying d_U | p_i. Consequently, on any hypothetical linear-length active second-shell phase, the four causal layers obey the strict order shadow-blocking < coatom-blocking < root-release < root-service. Proof is a case split on the legality of every coatom-subfamily's common divisor, using that a move first making d_U illegal must divide it.

- Prime-band recursive factorization (largest-prime-factor decomposition of the board): write P^+(x) for the largest prime factor of x. For bands I_j = (n^{1/(j+1)}, n^{1/j}], let beta_j(n) = log((j+1)/j) + o(1) be the band's reciprocal prime mass (Mertens). Every integer <= n has at most j prime factors, counted with multiplicity, exceeding n^{1/(j+1)}, so the product j*beta_j(n) = j*log(1+1/j) + o(1) = 1 - 1/(2j) + O(j^{-2}) is strictly less than 1 for every fixed j, and prod_{j<=J} [j*log(1+1/j)] =~ J^{-1/2}. Proof is direct counting of large-prime-factor multiplicity against Mertens' theorem on the band. This is a genuine static deficit on the projection multiplicity of a single move (see "What does not work" for the limits of this fact). Alongside it, four further unconditional results along the same decomposition:
  (i) Private-chain lemma: if N_j single-root gadgets are swept by Shortener, with each Prolonger reply advancing at most one gadget and each advanced gadget producing at most one child, then N_{j+1} <= (N_j+1)/2, so sum_j N_j <= 2N_0 + O(depth); entering an n/H-fortress of N_0 = n/H private chains of length H therefore yields only O(n/H) resolutions, not a linear number.
  (ii) Tagged-cone protection lemma: for C_d = {dx : 1 <= x <= n/d}, if y | x, dx is legal, and the intended move dy is illegal, then every selected move a comparable to dy that is responsible for that illegality satisfies d | a; hence a Shortener strategy lifted from a quotient board by multiplying every move by d cannot be spoiled by an untagged selected move, regardless of play order.
  (iii) Clean prime-fibre lemma: for distinct primes q, q' > sqrt(n), no member of F_q = {qm : 1 <= m <= n/q} divides a member of F_{q'} = {q'm : 1 <= m <= n/q'}; the board above sqrt(n) decomposes into genuinely independent quotient games of aggregate mass (log 2 + o(1))n.
  (iv) Fixed finite gadgets cannot give a linear lower bound: divisor cubes over a fixed prime set with pairwise-coprime cores require pairwise-coprime cores for privacy against a single external rough divisor, and a pairwise-coprime subset of [1, N] has size O(pi(N) + sqrt(N)) = o(N), reproducing only the known n/log n-type population.

- Empirical evidence (finite n, not a proof of any asymptotic statement): a sieve-based simulation of the game on {2,...,n} for n in {2000, 5000, 10000, 20000, 50000}, with Shortener playing the divisibility-minimal maximum-degree policy on the second shell S_1 = (n/4, n/2] (activation threshold eta = 0.25), run against three Prolonger policies -- uniform-random-legal, greedy-block (maximizing the number of currently-legal small divisors of the reply), and shell-defender (playing S_1 elements directly) -- found that no tested policy sustains an S_1-active phase proportional to n: the active-phase fraction shrinks as n grows (strongest policy, greedy-block: 0.012 at n=2000 down to 0.0058 at n=50000; the other two policies an order of magnitude smaller and also shrinking), and the small-prime demand of the service roots also shrinks relative to both n and to n*log log Y. This is consistent with second-shell clearing holding against these three adversaries at the tested scales; it is not a proof in either direction, since three heuristic policies are not an optimal adversary.

- Small-n exact values (verified minimax recurrence): writing f(n) for the exact game length on {2,...,n} under optimal play, f(n) - pi(n) = 0 for n <= 8, 1 for 9 <= n <= 20, 2 for 21 <= n <= 27, 3 for 28 <= n <= 34, 4 for 35 <= n <= 40. For example f(11) = 6: the chain decomposition {2,4,8}, {3,6}, {5,10}, {7}, {9}, {11} (six chains) gives Dilworth maximum antichain size 6, and Prolonger's opening move 6 forces every maximal extension to contain {6, 7, 9, 11} plus one of {4, 8} plus one of {5, 10}, so |A| >= 6.

## What does not work, and why it fails

- Ambient shadow-capacity inequality (SC*): fix alpha in (0, 1/4), a constant C_alpha, a deterministic cutoff Z_n -> infinity with Z_n <= n/4, and Lambda_n = sum_{p <= Z_n} 1/p. Let E(I) be the realized degree ledger over an active interval I and let S_{alpha,Z_n}(I) be the small-prime demand of service roots g_t >= alpha*n in I. The statement (SC*)(alpha) -- that S_{alpha,Z_n}(I) <= C_alpha*E(I) + o(n*Lambda_n) uniformly over every realized interval I -- is EQUIVALENT, for every fixed alpha > 0, to the statement it was proposed as a stepping stone toward: no admissible linear capped second-shell tail exists. Mechanism: a uniform Turan-Kubilius plus Cauchy-Schwarz bound gives (SC*)(alpha) automatically, with C_alpha = 0, whenever the macroscopic service-root cohort size is uniformly o(n) over realized intervals; conversely, a positive-density cohort forces demand of order n*Lambda_n for every admissible cutoff and every finite C_alpha. So the bare truth value of (SC*) is exactly the truth value of second-shell clearing, obtained without ever invoking the degree ledger's first-blocker structure, coatom preparation, downward-shadow closure, or the four-layer causal chronology above. It supplies no independent content as an intermediate target.

- Relative repair (SC^rel): replacing the ambient n*Lambda_n denominator with a remainder scaled by the realized ledger E(I)*Lambda_n plus a frozen baseline budget B_{alpha,n} (chosen so B_{alpha,n} = o(n*Lambda_n)) is EQUALLY circular. For every fixed alpha, (SC^rel)(alpha) is equivalent to the same no-linear-active-tail statement, via a slow-cutoff degree-dilution mechanism: on sublinear-ledger intervals a diverging sparse second-shell maximum-degree modulus lets a sufficiently slow deterministic cutoff Z_n prove (SC^rel) with no appeal to chronology, shadow closure, packet lcms, or first-death ledgers at all; on non-sublinear-ledger intervals the same Turan-Kubilius plus Cauchy-Schwarz absorption used for the ambient inequality applies. Both the ambient and relative capacity-inequality formulations therefore reduce exactly to the finite-shell-clearing target; proving either is exactly as hard as proving the underlying statement directly, not a step toward it.

- Tagged tensorization: the claim that a single Prolonger move inherited by k largest-prime-tagged quotient boards can be charged, additively, to at most k ordinary quotient-move costs -- needed to turn the per-band deficit j*log(1+1/j) < 1 above into a multiplicative trajectory potential across bands -- is FALSE on the reading required for that chaining. The failure is on Shortener's (the responder's) side, not Prolonger's: the factor j correctly bounds how many quotient boards a single Prolonger move projects into, but for a single k-tag move, min over one compatible GLOBAL reply s of sum_p F_p(s/p) can exceed sum_p [min over independent LOCAL replies z_p of F_p(z_p)] by an amount tending to infinity. An explicit counterexample occurs already in the band I_3 = (n^{1/4}, n^{1/3}]: there exist distinct primes p < q in I_3, a first Prolonger move a with exactly the two I_3-tags p, q, and predetermined tagged packets of size M =~ n^{3/10}/log n, such that in the p-quotient and q-quotient boards separately, Shortener has an immediate legal reply that deletes an entire still-unexposed packet in each; but those two optimal quotient replies cannot be realized by one global legal move, and after every global Shortener reply, Prolonger has a legal second move after which at least 3M - O(1) tagged targets are protected (every future legal move comparable with at most one of them), versus only 2M + O(1) such targets if the quotient replies had been genuinely independent -- a compatibility-defect gap of at least M - O(1) -> infinity. Consequently j*log(1+1/j) < 1 is only a static per-move projection-multiplicity bound, not by itself a trajectory potential, and the band product prod_{j<=J} j*log(1+1/j) =~ J^{-1/2} does not by itself yield any o(n) rate. A surviving o(n) argument built on this frame would need a genuinely non-tensor, asynchronous cross-band reply-compatibility potential that tracks the cumulative weighted reply-compatibility defect globally, not an additive per-band charge -- the ordinary tagged quotient values a/p_i cannot supply it.

- Static Carrier Capacity Bound (the claim that for every legal upper prefix P of size epsilon*n, sum_{p in B(P)} 1/p <= C(epsilon) for some finite C(epsilon)): false. The top epsilon*n consecutive integers in (n/2, n] form a legal primitive set containing a multiple of every prime p <= epsilon*n, forcing sum 1/p >= sum_{p <= epsilon*n} 1/p = log log(epsilon*n) + O(1) -> infinity. No finite-prime "vaccination" (excluding any fixed prime set Q in advance) rescues it: an arithmetic progression with common difference prod_{q in Q} q reproduces the same divergence.

- Sub-minimax lower-bound constructions do not amplify to L(n) = Omega(n). A growing-cap fortress entry (reaching, in o(n) moves, a position where the degree stays below every prescribed H(n) -> infinity for a linear number of further turns) gives only L(n) >= c*n/H(n) = o(n). The rank-k fan hierarchy gives L(n) >= c_k*n(log log n)^{k-1}/log n with c_k decaying doubly exponentially in k, which is blocked from reaching linearity by letting k grow with n. Defeating one specific named Shortener policy is not a game-theoretic lower bound at all, since Shortener may adopt a different policy. A genuine disproof requires epsilon > 0 and a Prolonger strategy achieving epsilon*n against EVERY Shortener strategy along an infinite sequence n_k -> infinity; none of the above three constructions meets that quantifier.

- Diagonal amplification from growing-cap entry to a fixed-cap lower bound requires the UNIVERSAL hypothesis that entry is available for every H(n) -> infinity, not merely for one particular H_*(n) -> infinity: only under the universal quantifier does a staircase/diagonal argument yield constants H_0, c > 0 with entry at the fixed cap H_0, hence L(n) >= c*n via an absorbing-fortress potential. No entry theorem at any cap -- growing or fixed -- is actually established; treating a grant of entry at a single H(n) -> infinity as if it licensed the diagonal amplification is a quantifier error, not a valid derivation.

- Targeted trajectory statements proposed as sufficient conditions for L(n) = o(n) repeatedly collapse via a terminal-delay degenerate witness: a Shortener policy that postpones activation of a shell-block condition until the terminal position (where every shell is empty, every cap is 0, and the condition holds vacuously) satisfies the literal statement with zero content. Once repaired to require activation within o(n) moves -- ruling out the vacuous terminal witness -- both a post-peel completion statement and an ancestor-harvest statement were shown to become EXACTLY EQUIVALENT to L(n) = o(n) itself: proving the repaired statement is a full proof of the sublinear side, and disproving it is a positive-density lower bound along a subsequence. Such statements are restatements of the full problem, not intermediate stepping stones toward it.
