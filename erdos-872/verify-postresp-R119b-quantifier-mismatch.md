## B — **VALID for the exact baseline-complete statement; the reachability gloss is false**

Part IV still refutes the exact \(\mathrm{RMEAH}^{\sharp}(\mathbf K^\star)\). Document II does not rescue that assertion because the two documents quantify over fundamentally different sets of positions.

### 1. The exact quantifier mismatch

Suppressing the other parameters, Document I asserts

\[
\exists \Sigma\;
\forall A\in\mathcal B_n\;
\forall\Pi\;
P(\Sigma,A,\Pi),
\tag{1}
\]

where \(P\) includes \((\mathrm{CAP})\).

The baseline \(A\) is not generated by \(\Sigma\). After \(\Sigma\) has been selected, the universal quantifier may hand it any \(A\in\mathcal B_n\), including an off-policy position that could never arise when \(\Sigma\) is used from the empty board.

Document II proves a statement of the form

\[
\exists \Gamma\;
\forall\Pi\;
\forall t,\qquad
A_t^{\Gamma,\Pi}\notin\mathcal F_{c,H_0},
\tag{2}
\]

where \(\mathcal F_{c,H_0}\) is the family of linear, \(H_0\)-capped trapped reservoirs and, in the growing-cap version, \(H_0(n)=o(r_{\mathrm{DA}}(n))\).

Statements (1) and (2) are perfectly compatible. Equation (2) says that \(\Gamma\) avoids the fortress on its own trajectories. Equation (1) requires \(\Sigma\) to work even when initialized after some other legal history has already built the fortress.

“Obtainable” in Part IV therefore means only

\[
\exists\text{ a legal history ending at }A_n^\star,
\]

not

\[
\forall\Sigma\;\exists\Pi
\text{ forcing }A_n^\star\text{ against }\Sigma.
\]

Document II disproves the second interpretation for its clearing policy. Part IV never proves forceability against that policy.

### 2. Nevertheless, arbitrary-baseline CAP is still contradicted

Baseline completeness explicitly places the accepted primorial-batched positions in \(\mathcal B_n\). Thus, after seeing an arbitrary candidate strategy \(\Sigma\), the universal baseline quantifier may select \(A_n^\star\).

At that supplied baseline,

\[
q_n\ge cn,\qquad D_n(0)\le H_0(n)\le H,
\]

so \((\mathrm{CAP})\) begins immediately.

For the next

\[
T_n\le 2B_\delta(q_n)+1
=
O_\delta\!\left(n\frac{\log H}{H^2}\right)
\]

actual moves, the fortress erosion estimate gives

\[
q_n-|Q_n^{T_n}|
\le
H_0T_n+2T_n+o(n).
\]

Since \(H_0\le H\),

\[
H_0T_n
=
O_\delta\!\left(
n\frac{H_0\log H}{H^2}
\right)
=o(n),
\]

and \(T_n=o(n)\). Hence

\[
|Q_n^{T_n}|=q_n-o(n),
\]

uniformly over both players’ continuation choices. This contradicts

\[
|Q_n^{T_n}|
\le
\frac12q_n+\frac{n}{h^2}
=
\frac12q_n+o(n).
\]

In fact, for this particular baseline the failure has the stronger form

\[
\forall\Sigma\;\forall\Pi,\qquad
\neg\mathrm{CAP}(\Sigma,A_n^\star,\Pi).
\]

That is enough to negate (1).

### 3. Why the clearing sweep cannot be started after the baseline

Document II’s sweep is preventive, not curative. It uses high-degree moves before a capped linear reservoir has been established.

Once the universal baseline quantifier places play at \(A_n^\star\), every legal lower move affecting the fortress has degree at most \(H_0\). Running “maximum degree” from that point merely selects a move of degree at most \(H_0\); it cannot recreate the earlier high-degree clearing regime. The erosion estimate proves that no post-entry policy can satisfy the short CAP deadline.

Thus Part IV survives not because the statement forbids an otherwise successful pre-entry sweep, but because the statement skips over the prehistory and demands a rapid cure from an already fortified state.

### 4. What happens under trajectory-restricted quantifiers

Suppose the statement were changed to

\[
\exists\Sigma\;
\forall\Pi\;
\forall A\in\operatorname{Reach}(\Sigma,\Pi),\qquad
P(\Sigma,A,\Pi).
\tag{3}
\]

Then, assuming \(H_0(n)=o(r_{\mathrm{DA}}(n))\), Document II’s clearing strategy can exclude \(A_n^\star\) from the quantified domain. Part IV would no longer have a counterexample.

Document I does not prove that its scheduling, clock, or high-degree obligations prevent such a clearing policy from running. In the exact formulation those questions are moot because the policy starts after the arbitrary baseline. In a formulation like (3), they would require a new incompatibility proof. None is supplied.

So the precise adjudication is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{The fortress is not reachable against Document II's clearing policy;}\\
\text{nevertheless Part IV validly refutes the exact universal-baseline statement;}\\
\text{Part IV does not refute a trajectory-restricted replacement.}
\end{array}}
\]

## A — **REPAIRABLE, but Parts I–II are not presently nondegenerate**

The direct cap-escape loophole is blocked syntactically by \((\mathrm{CAP})\). But the claimed repair of epoch-boundary laundering is incomplete, and there are additional scheduling and clock escapes.

### Eleventh witness: birth-at-the-boundary laundering

The decisive definitions are

\[
\xi(\omega)
=
\min\{t\ge s_{X,j}: \max_c I_c(\cdots,\mathfrak h_t)=1\}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Anc}_{X,j}(t)
=
\sum_{\substack{
s_{X,j}<\xi(\omega)\le t\\
\chi(\omega)=\mathrm{anc}
}}
w_{X,j}(\omega).
\]

Consider an epoch-\(j\) atom whose obstruction was created before \(s_{X,j}\).

There are two possibilities.

**Persistent predicates.** If the inherited obstruction makes

\[
I_{\mathrm{anc}}(A,X,\omega,\mathfrak h_{s_{X,j}})=1,
\]

then

\[
\xi(\omega)=s_{X,j}.
\]

But the strict inequality \(s_{X,j}<\xi(\omega)\) in (L6) excludes its weight permanently. It is never counted in \(\operatorname{Anc}_{X,j}(t)\).

**Instantaneous-event predicates.** If \(I_c\) is true only at the move on which the relevant spoiler was played, then that time lies before \(s_{X,j}\). Because the minimization starts at \(s_{X,j}\), the earlier event is never found; typically \(\xi(\omega)=\infty\).

Either interpretation loses inherited debt.

The constraints (L3)–(L4) do not repair this. They say that epoch atoms are disjoint and that one target receives at most \(J_H\) total weight across epochs. They do not require a new epoch atom’s inherited obstruction to have been represented by an old atom, nor do they carry the old setup cost into the new epoch inequality.

A formal witness can satisfy all four ledger conditions:

- one unit-weight new atom for each surviving target;
- disjoint epoch labels;
- at most one copy per target in this epoch, hence well below \(J_H\);
- every new atom already obstructed by an old spoiler.

All that mass is omitted by (L6). Thus the claimed cure for epoch-boundary laundering has the same temporal inheritance defect in a new location.

A repair would require either:

\[
\xi^{\mathrm{glob}}(\omega)
=
\min\{t\ge\text{original baseline}:I_c(\cdots,\mathfrak h_t)=1\},
\]

together with a consistent allocation of globally triggered atoms to epochs, or an explicit carried-debt term at every epoch start. Merely pre-registering labels is insufficient.

### Twelfth witness: an open epoch has no time budget

The charged clock is

\[
\ell_{X,j}(t)=\sum_i\rho_{X,j,i},
\]

but there is no axiom of the form

\[
\#\{\text{Shortener service turns in epoch }j\}
\le C\ell_{X,j}(\tau_{X,j})+O(1),
\tag{4}
\]

nor any bound on the elapsed duration \(\tau_{X,j}-s_{X,j}\).

The text only requires moves “used by the canonical accounting” to have \(\rho=1\). That leaves two escapes:

1. Shortener can leave an epoch open while making uncharged moves elsewhere.
2. Targets can be removed slowly through moves accounted for only by the area term \(\mathsf A_{X,j}\), without those moves necessarily incrementing \(\ell\).

Consequently, halving may take \(\Theta(m_{X,j})\) actual turns while

\[
\ell_{X,j}(\tau_{X,j})
=
O\!\left(m_{X,j}\frac{\log h}{h}\right)
\]

or even zero.

This invalidates the step in Part III asserting that the number of Shortener service turns is

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{\log h}{h}\sum_jm_{X,j}
\right).
\]

B1 bounds a selected event count, not service time. A fair scheduler and a rule charging every active-block Shortener turn are needed.

This also reopens a form of terminal delay: the ledger may exist from the beginning, but nothing requires the strategy to reach the halving/cap endpoint quickly.

### Literal CAP scheduling conflict

Clause (C1) applies “for every time \(s\)” and every shell \(X\), and requires Shortener to “immediately enter an exclusive capped block.”

If two shells satisfy (C1) at the same time, the next Shortener move is required to belong exclusively to both blocks. Moreover, while a capped block remains active, the same predicate remains true at later times, apparently retriggering new blocks.

The fixed symbol \(\operatorname{Sched}\) does not solve this because no fairness, serialization, pending-queue, or non-reentrance axioms are stated.

A coherent version needs:

- triggering only on cap entry or after completion of the previous block;
- one active exclusive block at a time;
- a defined queue for simultaneous triggers;
- a bound on waiting time or a global amortized waiting budget.

### History-versus-position ambiguity

The outer data are said to be functions on finite legal histories, but \(\mathcal B_n\) contains positions \(A\), and the ledgers are written as \(\Omega_{X,j}(A)\). The epoch start \(s_{X,j}\), previous exact-only creation, turn parity, and scheduler state can depend on which history reached the same set \(A\).

Either baselines must be finite histories, or every relevant map must be proved positional. As written, the codomain

\[
\pi_{X,j}:\Omega_{X,j}(A)\to Q_X^{s_{X,j}}
\]

can vary with the continuation even though its notation only takes \(A\).

### A verdict

The defects are fixable at the formulation level by using global exit times/carried debt, a serialized fair scheduler, and an actual-turn-to-clock domination such as (4). Those repairs do not undermine the fortress contradiction in Part IV; they merely produce a coherent statement for Part IV to refute.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{A: REPAIRABLE, not VALID.}}
\]

The cap-escape repair is present, but epoch-boundary laundering is not genuinely closed, and the charged clock is not yet a processing-time budget.

## C — **REFUTED**

Even granting Parts I–II after repair and granting Part IV completely, the advertised family-wide no-go theorem does not follow.

Part IV proves a much narrower proposition:

> No policy can rapidly halve every arbitrary baseline-complete capped fortress within the specific budget
> \[
> B_\delta(q)=O\!\left(q\frac{\log h}{h}\right).
> \]

It does not prove that every sufficient ancestor-based formulation must demand such a cure from every off-policy fortress baseline.

### The trichotomy omits prevention

Part V’s three cases omit the logically distinct possibility

\[
\boxed{
\text{The chosen policy prevents a large capped state from being reached.}
}
\]

For example, a trajectory invariant could assert

\[
D_X(t)\le H
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|Q_X^t|\le \theta_X
\tag{5}
\]

for every state reached under the chosen policy, where \(\theta_X\) is already dust-sized.

Equation (5) is not a CAP halving theorem. It says the problematic capped block never begins with large mass.

Document II supplies exactly the fixed-density prototype of this architecture: for every fixed \(c>0\), its sweep prevents entry into a capped reservoir of size \(cn\). It does not yet give the shrinking-density uniformity needed for full OA, but that limitation concerns whether the required prevention theorem is presently known—not whether the logical fourth case exists.

Thus Case 1 of Part V is false as a family-wide statement. Exempting capped states is harmless when another policy invariant proves that no large exempt state is reachable.

### The statement’s own obligations are not shown to exclude prevention

To restore the no-go argument, Document I would need a theorem saying:

> Every Shortener policy satisfying the high-degree ancestor, budget, and scheduling requirements necessarily permits Prolonger to create a linear \(H_0=o(r_{\mathrm{DA}})\)-capped fortress.

No such result appears. “Obtainable under some play” is far weaker.

Depending on how \(\operatorname{Sched}\) and \(\rho\) are repaired, a clearing sweep might be compatible or incompatible with the ancestor obligations. That must be proved, not assumed.

### “Projection equals OA” is not a no-go argument

Part V argues that if a trajectory-restricted theorem includes capped contraction, then projecting away the ledgers yields OA, so the theorem is not a “proper intermediate” result.

That is not a mathematical contradiction. An intermediate structural theorem can imply the target statement after auxiliary variables are eliminated; what matters is whether its hypotheses and conclusion can be established independently.

More importantly, a prevention invariant like (5) does not insert the CAP conclusion at all. The capped branch is absent because its large-state antecedent is unreachable.

### Other unexcluded architectures

The proposed trichotomy also fails to cover:

- a global potential decrease rather than shell-by-shell immediate halving;
- a cap threshold depending on current reservoir density;
- longer processing for rare capped states, compensated by a global amortized budget;
- a static “clearability certificate” proved for all baselines satisfying that certificate, plus a policy theorem maintaining the certificate;
- mixed ancestor and degree-availability arguments that force halving before cap attainment.

Whether any of these can be proved is separate. Part IV does not eliminate them.

### The strongest justified no-go statement

A valid conclusion would be:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
No assertion that is uniform over every baseline in the stated
baseline-complete class can require every linear capped fortress to
halve within \(O(q\log h/h)\) Shortener turns.
\end{minipage}}
\]

That is a genuine and useful obstruction.

The broader conclusion

\[
\text{“no baseline-independent, ancestor-harvest-only separator of this family”}
\]

requires a formal definition of the family and an exhaustiveness theorem. Neither is supplied, and Document II exposes the missing prevention architecture.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{C: REFUTED.}}
\]
